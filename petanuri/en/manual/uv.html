UV pane and visible area

Place the UV (the unwrapped layout of the texture) next to the 3D view and paint from either one. For hidden surfaces, such as the body under clothing or the inside of hair, hide the front surfaces with the visible area and paint.

Screenshots show the Japanese interface. Menu names in the text follow the selected app language.

Start with this
  1. Press U to place the UV next to the 3D view. You can paint right on the UV too (details).
  2. Use "Paint area" at the bottom left of the UV pane to choose between painting only inside islands or out into the padding (details).
  3. To paint hidden surfaces, select islands or faces and press Shift+H (show selected only) or H (hide selected). Alt+H brings everything back (details).
  4. Overlapping cloth can be hidden by tracing it with the "Hide pen" (details).
  5. Press Alt+W (Seam lines) to show the positions of UV seams in 3D (details).

Show UV and paint directly

In the UV pane you can paint directly on the unwrapped texture. Use it for places that are hard to reach in 3D, or where you want to draw a straight line.

  1. Press U (or click "UV" at the right end of the 3D view, or use Display → UV → UV (side by side with 3D)). The UV pane appears next to the 3D view.
  2. Drag the divider to adjust the widths.
  3. Draw in the UV pane the same way as in 3D.
  • The UV shown is that of the selected texture set (its name is at the top left). It switches when you select a different set (Choose the set to draw on).
  • Tools you can use: Pen, Brush, Eraser, Polygon fill, Fill, Fill enclosed area, Gradient, Shape, Stamp, Eyedropper, Blur and Smudge.
  • While you work in the UV pane, the faces inside the brush circle glow red in the 3D view. While you work in 3D, the painting position appears as a red dot in the UV pane.
  • "Symmetry X, Y, Z" in the header is the same switch as at the top left of the 3D view. Even when you paint in the UV, it also paints the spot mirrored along the same 3D axis (Paint symmetrically).
  • At the bottom right, a small UV of the Sub texture set appears. Click it to make the Sub the Main. Hide it with ✕ and bring it back with "Show sub" (Main and Sub).

Switch the layout (3D only, side by side, UV only, small window)

LayoutWhat to do
3D and UV side by sideU, or "UV" at the right end of the 3D view. When only the UV is shown, "3D" at the left end of the UV pane
3D onlyThe ✕ at the top right of the UV pane (or press U again)
UV onlyThe ✕ at the top right of the 3D view, or Shift+U (Display → UV → UV only (hide 3D))
Make one of them a small windowWhile they are side by side, the button under the ✕ at the top right. You can also use Display → UV → "UV in a small window (3D full)" or "3D in a small window (UV full)"
  • The layout you used last is the same the next time you open it.
  • A small window can also be placed over the panels, as long as it stays within the app window. Drag its header to move it, and drag the four edges or corners to resize it.
  • In the small window, ⇄ swaps it with the full view, ▣ returns to the side-by-side layout, and ✕ closes the small window. The lock at the top right fixes it in place (click again to release).
  • Even if you hide the panels with Tab, the small window stays. The 2D canvas can't use a small window.

Show UV lines, island edges and padding lines

  • The "UV lines", "Island edges" and "Padding line" buttons in the header show or hide the UV lines, the island outlines, and the line that lies outside the island edge by the padding width (Padding).
  • The ▼ next to them changes the color and opacity (5 to 100%) of the three lines. "Reset to default color" restores the originals.
  • The right-click menu of the UV pane has the same switches.
  • To turn the UV lines into an image to use as a guide in other software, use "Export UV map as PNG" (details).

View a normal map folder by height

Pro When the paint target is the normal map, the UV pane normally shows the normal map's colors. If you turn on 3D right-click → Display → "Show the normal map folder as height (gray) (UV)", it shows the height you painted, in gray. White is raised and black is carved (paint targets).

If it doesn't work

  • I can't find a header button: If the UV pane is narrow, buttons that don't fit are put away under "⋯". Click "⋯" to show them.
  • The UV doesn't appear: You can show it once a 3D model is open. Open a model first.
  • There are areas I can't paint: Faces whose UV collapses to a single point, faces that extend outside 0 to 1, and spots where more than 4 faces overlap can't be painted. A notice appears at the bottom right when you open the model.
  • Islands are placed outside 0 to 1: PetaNuri3D maps them to the position inside 0 to 1 that gives the same color through texture repeat, and paints there. The model file is not changed.

Move the UV view (zoom, pan, rotate)

You can move the UV pane with a mouse or with only a pen. The guide at the top right has the same form as the 3D navigator.

Show everythingThe square on the guide, or "Frame all UV" in the right-click menu

Rotate to any angleDrag the rotation button on the guide left or right, or hold Space+Shift and drag in the pane
Reset the rotationClick the angle on the guide (double-clicking the rotation button also returns it to 0°)
Set a fixed zoomCtrl+1, 2, 3 (100%, 200% and 50% at first)
  • You can keep painting while the view is rotated.
  • At 100%, one texture pixel is one screen pixel. Change the zoom levels in File → Preferences → Controls, "Zoom presets (UV / 2D)". While a 2D canvas is open, it applies to the canvas. It doesn't work in the 3D view.
  • Drag the handle at the top of the guide to move it anywhere, and resize it from the left and bottom edges and the bottom left corner. "—" collapses it into a small round icon, and clicking the icon restores it (you can also use Display → UV → UV guide (zoom/move)).
  • If you turn on "Show rotation/zoom sliders on the navigator" in File → Preferences → Interface → Display, sliders appear below the guide (and on the 3D navigator too).

Grid and snapping

Show a grid to line up the positions of patterns and emblems. When you move a frame, it snaps to the grid lines.

  1. Click "Grid" in the header of the UV pane. The grid appears.
  2. Choose "Cells" from the ▼ next to it. It divides each side of the texture into 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64 or 128.
  3. When you move the frame of a move or transform, its four corners or its center snap to nearby grid lines.
  • The ▼ shows how many px one cell is (dividing 2048 px by 16 gives 128 px).
  • The lines are display only. They are not part of your painting, your saves or your exports. They don't get thicker when you zoom in.
  • "Snap to the grid when moving/transforming" is on from the start. It works only while the grid is shown.
  • Snapping applies to the frames of the Move/transform tool, Transform (Ctrl+T) and Paste image onto UV. The corner and edge handles, the corners of Free transform and the points of Warp snap as well (Transform).
  • It doesn't snap while Ctrl is held. A frame over the 3D pane doesn't snap either.
  • You can also show it from Display → UV → "UV grid". You can assign a key to turning snapping on and off (Shortcuts).

Paint area and padding (paint outside islands)

If you spread the edge color slightly outside the UV islands, lines are less likely to appear at seams in the game. This extra band is called padding. Use the bottom left of the UV pane to choose how far you paint.

  1. Under "Paint area" at the bottom left of the UV pane, click "In UV", "In padding" or "Everywhere".
  2. For "In padding", set the padding width (0 to 128 px) with the slider below.
  3. Paint in the UV pane.
Paint areaWhere you can paint
In UV (default)Only inside UV islands. "Paint across seams" is available (details)
In paddingInside islands and the padding band outside them. The Pen, shapes and so on spill onto neighboring islands too
EverywhereAnywhere on the texture. Paint spills outside islands and onto neighboring islands
  • Besides the Pen, Brush, Eraser and Polygon fill, Shape, Fill, Gradient, Stamp, Blur and Smudge also follow this area. Painting in the 3D view is unchanged.
  • With "In padding" and "Everywhere", Fill spreads the painted area outside the islands.
  • The padding value is the same value as the export bleed ("Shared with export"). Changing either one changes both. "All" is the same as "Fill everything outside islands" in the export window (export bleed).
  • You can collapse the box at the bottom left with its ▼. Even when it is small, the paint area and "Paint across seams" still work.

Extend lines drawn in 3D into the padding too

Turn on "Paint into padding outside islands in 3D too" in the Tool properties of the Pen, Brush and Eraser. When you finish a stroke in 3D, the line is extended outside the island by the padding width.

  • It does not go into neighboring islands. A single undo removes the whole line.
  • The width is "Padding width (px)" in the Tool properties. It is the same value as the bottom left of the UV pane and the export bleed.
  • When it is off, the line is extended only slightly from the island edge.

Make a padding-only layer

Use Filters → "Create padding on a new layer" to put only the extra area onto a new layer. That extra area is the edge colors of the current image (all layers stacked), spread outside the islands. Inside the islands it is transparent, and the layer is placed right above the selected layer.

Faint areas at the edge of a brush stroke are not treated as painted, and the color of the opaque areas is spread outside the islands. Pixels outside the islands take the color of the nearest island pixel by straight-line distance, so the color spreads smoothly even from diagonal edges.

When strokes go beyond an island (paint across seams)

Some places are far apart in the UV but connected in 3D. Paint that spills over a seam can continue onto the face that is connected in 3D.

  • When you paint in the UV pane, even if the circle spills outside the island, it does not paint on another island that is merely next to it in the UV (but far away in 3D).
  • "Paint across seams" at the bottom left (on by default): paint that spills over a seam at an island edge continues onto the face connected beyond that seam in 3D. The line doesn't break at the seam.
  • If you turn it off, only the inside of the island you are drawing on is painted. The same switch is in the right-click menu as "Continue painting outside islands onto 3D-connected neighbors" and in Display → UV.
  • "Paint across seams" works only when the paint area is "In UV".

When you start drawing outside an island

  • When the paint area is "In UV", the brush circle turns into a faint dotted line with a × over any spot that no face uses. The Pen, Brush, Eraser and Polygon fill can't paint there.
  • If you start a line outside an island, it begins to take effect where it enters the island.
  • Text, shapes, stamps and gradients can be placed even if you start outside an island (color is applied only inside the island).

If it doesn't work

  • "Can't paint across seams on this layer" appears: A clipping layer (and its mask) paints to match the shape of the layer below, so it doesn't continue beyond a seam.
  • I can't click "Paint across seams": Set the paint area to "In UV".

Visible area (show or hide only selected parts)

The body under clothing or the inside of hair is blocked by the surfaces in front, so you can't paint it. If you hide the front surfaces with the visible area, the surfaces behind show as they are and you can paint them.

  1. Select islands or faces in the UV pane or in 3D (with the Selection tool's "Select by island" or "Select by face", the lasso, vertex groups and so on).
  2. Press Shift+H (or use the Visible area icon at the top left of the 3D view, or "Show selected only" in the UV header). Only what you selected appears in 3D.
  3. To hide only the front, press H ("Hide selected"). You can keep adding more to hide.
  4. When you finish painting, press Alt+H to show everything again.
ActionWhat it does
Show selected only (Shift+H)Shows only the selected islands or faces. While it is on, selecting something else changes the display without pressing it again. Press it again to show everything
Hide selected (H)Hides the selected islands or faces. Hiding releases the selection, so you can paint straight on what is visible
Also show selectedBrings the selected part of what you hid back into view
Invert visible areaSwaps what is visible with what is hidden
Expand, ContractExpands or contracts the visible area on the UV by the number of px you set (1 to 64)
Show all (Alt+H)Brings back everything you hid, including hidden objects
  • All of these are in Display → Visible area, and in the "Visible area" item of the 3D and UV right-click menus.
  • You can restore a selection that was released with "Reselect" in the bar under the 3D view, or with Ctrl+Shift+D.
  • Hidden areas show diagonal stripes in the UV pane. While something is hidden, "Show all" is highlighted.
  • You can also hide by object (mesh) (Show or hide objects).

Hide or show only the island you click (right-click)

  • "Hide this island" and "Show only this island" in the 3D right-click menu hide or show the island you clicked, as it is. The selection and the undo history don't change.
  • "Select this island (selection)" in the 3D right-click menu makes the island you clicked the selection.
  • The right-click menu of the UV pane has "Select this island (selection)", "Add this island to selection", "Show only this island in 3D" and "Hide this island in 3D". These select the island first and then show or hide it, so the selection changes.

The Visible area ▼ window

The ▼ under the Visible area icon at the top left of the 3D view opens a window that gathers the visible area operations. When nothing is hidden, you work from here.

  • Upper left, "From selection": Show selected only, Hide selected, Also show selected. Under "Directly" is the Hide pen.
  • Lower left, "Hidden parts": Invert, Show all, the amount (px) with Expand and Contract, and Dim surroundings.
  • Right, "Hide presets": the name of the current hide preset and the list of saved ones (Save hide presets).
Hidden parts don't count for depth either, so the surfaces behind show as they are and you can paint them. You can't paint directly on hidden parts, and the Eyedropper and Fill don't pick them up. If you turn on "Mirror painting on hidden parts (3D brushes)" in the Symmetry ▼, the 3D Pen, Brush, Eraser and Stamp also paint symmetrically on the hidden opposite side (Paint symmetrically).
Hiding and showing are not recorded in the undo (Ctrl+Z) history. To go back, show all, or load a saved hide preset.

If it doesn't work

  • "Select islands or faces first" appears: Nothing is selected. Select in 3D or the UV pane with "Select by island" or "Select by face" of the Selection tool, or with the right-click "Select this island (selection)", and then press the button. The buttons in the UV header look faint until you select something.
  • Ctrl+Z doesn't restore the display: Hiding isn't in the history. Restore with Alt+H or with "Show all" in the bar under the 3D view.
  • The selection disappeared when I hid: Hiding releases the selection. Ctrl+Shift+D (Reselect) brings it back.
  • Part of the model doesn't appear: It is still hidden by the visible area or by object. Press Alt+H to show everything. The hidden state is saved in the project file, so it stays hidden even after you reopen the file.

Hide by tracing (Hide pen) and the bar under the 3D view

When you paint behind overlapping cloth, you can hide whatever you trace as it is. You don't need to make a selection first.

  1. Choose "Hide pen" in the toolbar on the left (you can also toggle it with "Hide pen" in the bar under the 3D view).
  2. In the Sub tool, choose "Hide by face" (a quad is hidden as its two triangles together) or "Hide by island" (a whole UV island: one piece of cloth, one part and so on).
  3. Trace in 3D, and what you traced is hidden. You can also click in the UV pane to hide.
  4. Click "Hide pen" again to go back to the previous tool.
One stroke doesn't hide the faces behind that become visible after you hide the front. If you want to hide those too, trace again.

The bar under the 3D view

The bar at the bottom center of the 3D view appears only while something is hidden and while the Hide pen is active.

  • "Hide pen", "Face | Island", "Hide presets ▼", "Reselect" and "Dim surroundings" are lined up with the current status and "Show all".
  • The status is one of "Hide preset: (name)" (unchanged from a saved hide preset), "Some parts hidden (unsaved hide preset)" or "Showing only the selected part". When objects are hidden, "n object(s) hidden" also appears.
  • Click "Show all" to bring everything back in one step.

Show hidden areas faintly

You can show hidden areas semi-transparent and paint while checking the surroundings.

  1. While something is hidden, click "Dim surroundings" in the bar under the 3D view. The hidden areas appear semi-transparent.
  2. Change the opacity (5 to 80%) with the slider beside it.
  • The same applies to "Dim surroundings" under "Hidden parts" in the Visible area ▼ window (0% shows nothing).
  • Even when they look faint, you can't paint directly on hidden areas.

Save hide presets

Give a name to a hide state you use often and keep it, so you can return to the same state any time.

  1. Hide the areas you want to hide.
  2. Click "Hide presets ▼" → "Save current hide preset" in the bar under the 3D view, and enter a name (the same name overwrites). You can also save from the Visible area ▼ window.
  3. Click a name in the same list to return to that hide state.
  • Use "Manage hide presets (rename, delete)" to rename or delete a preset.
  • Saved hide presets and the current hide state are saved in the project file (.petanuri). You can use them after you reopen the file.

Show mesh lines and seam lines

Shows the polygon lines and the UV seams (island edges) overlaid on the 3D model. They are a guide when you paint near seams.

  1. Click the line icon at the top left of the 3D view (or use Display → Lines & rulers → Mesh wireframe). The polygon lines appear.
  2. Show the seam lines with the button next to it or with Alt+W (Display → Lines & rulers → Seam lines (UV seams and island edges)).
  • The ▼ under the icon lets you choose whether to show lines, the opacity (5 to 100%), the color (blue, black, white), whether to also show back and hidden lines ("Also show back and hidden lines (see-through)"), and the seam lines.
  • Normally only the lines of visible faces are shown. In Single-sided display, the lines of back-facing faces aren't shown either.
  • The 3D right-click → Display has the same switches.
  • To hide the texture and view only the lines, use Display → Shading → "Wireframe only".
  • Seam lines are not the same as a layer's "Stroke" (border).

Paint along UV edges (Paint from UV edges) Pro

Creates a line or gradient on a new layer based on the distance from the edges of UV islands. Use it for trim along clothing edges, or for painting that is darker toward the edges.

  1. Choose Filters → "Paint from UV edges (new layer)". While the window is open, a preview line appears in the UV and in 3D.
  2. Choose "Shape" (line or gradient) and "Direction" (inside islands, outside islands, or both).
  3. Adjust "Width", "Start from edge", "Blur" and "Opacity". The preview line changes as soon as you change a value.
  4. Under "Color", choose the foreground color or "Choose a color", and click "Create". A new layer is created.
  • Distances are in texture px. A gradient is strongest at the edge and becomes transparent at the width.
  • Where islands touch each other, there is no edge. If there is a selection, the layer is created only inside it.
  • With "Inside islands", it also paints the area just outside the islands (the padding width) at the same strength as the nearest island pixel. Turn off "Also paint just outside islands (padding)" to avoid that.
  • With "Choose a color", you can choose from the swatches of the Color set or from the color field. For a transparent color, no new layer is created. It erases this edge from the current layer instead.
  • Cancel or Esc removes the preview line. After you create the layer, a single undo reverts it.

UV orientation for glTF, glb and VRM

glTF counts the top and bottom of an image the opposite way from FBX and similar formats. When PetaNuri3D opens a glTF, glb or VRM, it flips the UV vertically so that it matches FBX. This is the same orientation you get when you import into Blender or UniVRM.

  • Images contained in the model go into the base in their original orientation.
  • The picture in the UV pane, exported PNGs, PSDs and UV maps have the same orientation as the original image. They line up as they are in other 3D software.
  • Importing images and PSDs also brings them in as they are, without flipping.
