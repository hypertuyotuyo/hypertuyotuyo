Bake AO Pro

Calculates the areas that get dark because surrounding objects block the light (AO), such as under the arms, under the chin, under the bangs, between fingers and where clothing overlaps, and bakes them into a layer. It bakes with the current pose and blend shape as they are.

Screenshots show the Japanese interface. Menu names in the text follow the selected app language.

This is a Pro feature. In Lite the menu item has a lock icon, and in Standard it doesn't appear in the menu (Edition comparison).

Start with this
  1. Open Filters → Bake → "Bake AO (areas darkened by surroundings)" (Details).
  2. Check the texture sets to bake in the table, then click "Bake". An "AO" layer in Multiply mode is added at the top of each set (Details).
  3. If it's too dark, lower the opacity of the "AO" layer, or lower "Strength" and bake again (Details).
  4. To bake only part of the model, such as an accessory, choose it under "Objects" (Details).

Bake AO

This creates a picture in which the areas light can hardly reach are darkened, based on the shape of the model. Use it as a base for shading or when you want to add depth.

  1. Open Filters → Bake → "Bake AO (areas darkened by surroundings)".
  2. In the "Texture sets to bake" table, check the sets you want to bake. At first, the visible sets are checked.
  3. Choose "Quality" and "Shadow distance". If you're unsure, leave the defaults.
  4. Click "Bake". An "AO" layer in Multiply mode is added at the top of each set.
  5. Close the window with "Close" or ×.
  • You can still move the 3D view while baking. If you stop with "Cancel", nothing changes.
  • One Undo restores all sets.
  • Estimated time: about 30 seconds for an avatar with 6 texture sets at 2048 resolution, with the default settings (when calculated on the GPU).
  • For sets whose rendering mode is "Cutout" or "Transparent", light passes through the transparent parts of the picture (such as hair tips). "Opaque" ignores transparency (see Rendering mode).

Table of texture sets to bake

ColumnMeaning
BakeBakes AO into this set's texture (it receives shadows from other objects)
Cast shadowsThis set also casts shadows onto other sets. If you uncheck it, it casts shadows only onto itself
Ignore other shadowsDoes not receive shadows from other sets. Check this when something that is covered, such as the body under clothing, turns pitch black

For example, if you bake the body and set the hair and clothing to "Cast shadows" only, the shadows of the hair and clothing fall on the body.

Quality and shadow reach distance

ItemOptions
Rays64 to 4096 per texel (512 by default). More rays remove graininess but take longer
Within texel1×1 (center only, fast), 2×2, 4×4 (checks 16 spots, so edges are smooth; default)
DistanceNear (3 cm; only where surfaces touch, such as between fingers, collars and hairlines), Normal (8 cm; also under bangs, armpits, under the chin and skirt hems; default), Wide (20 cm; more realistic, so an arm also darkens the side of the body), Custom (the value in cm below)
Scale to the model's height (distance for a 1.5 m tall model)Makes the distance proportional to the model's height. The distance that actually applies is shown in the window

If it doesn't work

  • The body under the clothing turns pitch black: Check "Ignore other shadows" on the body's set and bake again.
  • Grain (noise) is noticeable: Increase "Rays", or set "Within texel" to 4×4. "Dither" in the detailed settings also makes the steps in the gradation less visible.
  • No light passes through the hair tips: Set the hair material's rendering mode to "Cutout" or "Transparent", then bake.
  • It isn't in the menu, or it has a lock icon: This is a Pro feature.

Bake only some objects

In the "Objects" section of the dialog, you can choose what to bake per object (mesh). For example, with the sample "Shinobu", choosing only furin bakes only the bell.

  1. In the "Objects" section, click "None" to uncheck everything.
  2. Check the objects you want to bake. Click a name to make it glow slightly in 3D.
  3. Choose how shadows are calculated, then click "Bake".
  • It writes only to the UV area of the selected objects (and a little beyond the islands). The areas of other objects in the same texture set stay as they are.
  • "Shadows from selected only" (default): calculates with only the shape of the selected objects. Shadows from other things, such as the body, are not included.
  • "Shadows from whole model": objects you didn't select also cast shadows. Areas close to the body get darker.
  • Sets that contain none of the selected objects are not baked. If all objects are selected, the whole model is baked.
  • Your selection is remembered per model.

Adjust darkness and quality

If the baked AO is too dark or too light, you can adjust it either after or before baking.

  • After baking: Lower the opacity of the "AO" layer. You can also use a mask or the Eraser to remove only the areas you don't need.
  • Before baking: Change the darkness with "Strength" (0 gives white) and "Contrast" under "Final look".
  • Use "Reset to defaults" to return the settings to their initial values (your choice of sets stays as it is).

Detailed settings

ItemDetails
FalloffSmooth (farther hits count less; default), Linear, None (same strength within the distance)
NormalSmooth (around the interpolated normal; default), Surface orientation
ComputeAuto (GPU, or CPU if unavailable), CUDA (NVIDIA GPU; app (exe) only, experimental), CPU (very slow)
Verify GPU results on the CPUChecks a small sample of the calculation results for each set
Meshes not in any set also cast shadowsMeshes that can't be painted (such as those with no UV) are also counted as objects that cast shadows
Outside islandsThe width (px) to extend outside the islands. If you choose "Auto", it is decided for you
Fill everything outside islandsKeeps the background from mixing into the edges even when the texture is shrunk
DitherMakes the steps in smooth gradations less visible
Offset from surfaceThe distance the starting point of each check is raised off the surface (mm, for a 1.5 m tall model)
Nearby translucencyTranslucent surfaces within this many mm are ignored (thin meshes such as cheek blush)
To make the white areas of the AO transparent and change the shadow color, you can use Convert luminance to opacity. To add soft shading based on surface orientation, or cast shadows from light, go to Easy Bake, material look and shading.

Output as lilToon Shadow AO or PNG

In the "Export" section of the dialog, you can choose where the baked AO goes. You can choose any number of them.

ItemWhere it goes
Add an "AO" layer (Multiply, top of the set)Bakes into the texture color (default)
Also write to lilToon's "Shadow AO" (AO Map)Adds the AO layer to the top of the material's "Shadow AO" paint target folder. The darker an area is, the more easily shadow appears there. It is output together with the Material export
Also save as PNG after baking (<set name>_AO.png)Saves an image of the AO alone
  • You can also save it later from Filters → Bake → "Export AO as PNG (last bake result)". Areas outside the islands are filled using the extension width in the AO settings (or the export bleed, if that is wider).
  • When writing to Shadow AO, if that set isn't using shadows, it is switched to "Use shadow" (Undo reverts this).
lilToon's AO map shifts the shadow boundary. If the shadow feels too strong, bake again with "Strength" at 0.6 to 0.8.
