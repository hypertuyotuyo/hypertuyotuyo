Selections, transforms, text and rulers

This page shows how to limit painting to a selection, move and reshape what you drew, place images and text on the model, and align lines with a ruler. The transform frame appears on the model in the 3D view and in the UV view.

Screenshots show the Japanese interface. Menu names in the text follow the selected app language.

Start with this
  1. Draw around an area with the "Selection" tool (S) and you can paint only inside it. Press Ctrl+D to deselect (details).
  2. With the "Move/transform" tool (V), click a drawing on the model and drag to move it (details).
  3. To resize or rotate, press Ctrl+T. Drag a corner to resize, drag the circle on top to rotate, and press Enter to apply (details).
  4. Drop an image onto the window, line up its frame, and paste it (details).
  5. Click the model with the "Text" tool (T) to type text right there (details).

Select the area to paint (Selection)

Once you make a selection, drawing, painting, erasing, filters and gradients affect only the inside of it. Use it when you want to paint without going over the edge.

  1. Select the "Selection" tool (S).
  2. In the sub tools, choose a selection method (Rectangle / Ellipse, Lasso, and so on).
  3. In the 3D view or UV view, drag, trace or click to select. The outside dims slightly and a selection outline appears along the boundary.
  4. Switch to a pen or another tool and paint. Nothing is applied outside the selection.
  5. Press Ctrl+D to deselect.

Sub tools and selection methods

Sub toolHow to select
Rectangle / EllipseDrag.
LassoTrace around the area.
PolylineClick to place points. Close the shape by clicking the first point, double-clicking, or pressing Enter.
Auto selectSelects the connected area of colors close to the point you click.
Select by islandSelects the whole UV island you click.
Select by faceSelects the whole faces you trace over.
Selection pen / Selection eraserTrace with a brush to add or subtract (reversed while you hold Alt).
  • In the 3D view, only visible faces are selected. Turn on "Also select back and hidden faces" in tool properties to select everything that overlaps the shape on screen, including the back. This is handy when you select all the way around the body to paint clothing stripes.
  • For Auto select, set "Reference" (All layers / Selected layer), "Tolerance" and "Anti-aliasing (smooth edges by 1px)" in tool properties.
  • For Select by face, set "Pick radius" and "Select by quads (treat 2 triangles as 1 face)".

Add to or subtract from a selection

In tool properties, choose New, Add, Subtract or Intersect under "Selection mode". If you hold a key before you start dragging, the mode changes for that one stroke only.

Key held at the startSelection mode
NoneNew (replaces the current selection). Click without moving to deselect
ShiftAdd (adds to the current selection)
AltSubtract (removes from the current selection)
Shift+AltIntersect (keeps only the overlap)

Other ways to select

  • Ctrl+click a layer thumbnail to select the painted area of that layer (Shift to add, Alt to subtract).
  • Right-click in the 3D view → "Select this island (selection)" to select the island you clicked. The right-click menu in the UV view also has "Add this island to selection".
  • To select all areas close to the foreground color at once, use Select all similar colors.

If it doesn't work

  • You can't paint where you want: A leftover selection blocks painting outside it. Press Ctrl+D to deselect.
  • You can't draw on other texture sets: While a selection exists, you can't draw on a texture set that has no selection. Either deselect, or use "Select all" (Ctrl+A), which covers every set.
  • The back isn't selected: In the 3D view, only visible faces are selected. Turn on "Also select back and hidden faces".
  • The outside is too dark to judge colors: Turn off Selection → "Darken outside the selection" and only the selection outline remains (menu).

Work with the selection bar

While a selection exists, the selection bar appears under the 3D view (under the UV view if only the UV is shown). Icon buttons follow the "Selection active" label on the left. Hold the pen tip or cursor over a button to see its name and key.

ButtonWhat it does
Deselect (the colored button)Removes the selection. Even after that, "Reselect" (Ctrl+Shift+D) brings it back
InvertInverts the selection
Expand, Contract, FeatherAdjust the shape of the selection. On a narrow screen they are grouped under "Modify ▾"
FillFills the selection with the foreground color
ClearClears the inside of the selection
To new layer ▾"Copy to new layer" and "Cut to new layer"
More ▾Create mask from selection, Show selected only, Hide selected, Show selection outline, Darken outside the selection
When the Hide pen bar or a ruler strip is showing, the selection bar sits above it. "Show selected only" and "Hide selected" are Visible area features.

The "Selection" menu lists the items for refining and using selections. Most of them are also in the "Selection" submenu of the right-click menu in the 3D view.

Select all similar colors

Selects areas close to the foreground color across the whole texture, including areas that aren't connected. Use it, for example, when you change the shadow color for each base color.

  1. With the Eyedropper (I), set the color you want to select as the foreground color.
  2. Choose Selection → "Select all similar colors (foreground color)".
  3. Use "Fuzziness (%)" to set how close a color must be to be selected.
  4. In "Sample from", choose "All layers" or "Current layer".
  5. In "Selection mode", choose "New", "Add" or "Subtract", then click "Choose".

Fill with one color and change colors in bulk

These are three items in the "Edit" menu. They affect the inside of the selection if there is one, or the whole selected layer if not.

ItemKeyWhat it does
Fill with foreground colorAlt+BackspaceFills with the foreground color
Fill with background colorCtrl+BackspaceFills with the background color
Change to foreground color (keep transparency)—Changes only the color of painted areas to the current foreground color. The density (transparency) and transparent areas stay as they are
  • "Change to foreground color" is for changing the color of line art in bulk. It is also in the right-click menu of a layer row.
  • When you are painting on a mask, the mask is filled according to the brightness of the color (white = show, black = hide).
  • Inside a normal map folder, it changes colors using gray. It can't be used on normal map image layers (Paint target).

Move what you drew (Move/transform tool)

Click a drawing on the 3D model and drag to move it. You don't have to look for the layer you drew on: the layer at the point you click is selected.

  1. Select the "Move/transform" tool (V).
  2. In the 3D view (or UV view), click the drawing you want to move. The topmost layer painted at that point is selected, and a frame appears around its artwork.
  3. Keep holding and drag, and it moves right away. Drag a corner to resize and the circle on top to rotate.
  4. Press Enter, or switch to another tool, to apply. Press Esc to cancel.

Detailed settings

  • Turn off "Auto-select layer" in tool properties to move the selected layer instead of the layer at the point you click.
  • Clicking outside the frame applies the move and switches to the layer at the point you clicked.
  • If a selection exists, only the inside of it moves. Applying counts as one Undo step.
  • In tool properties, use "Shape" to choose the frame type (Scale/rotate, Free transform or Warp) and "Warp grid" to choose the number of grid points (Free transform and Warp).
  • Clicking a text layer gives you the text frame. Drag inside the frame to move it, and double-click to retype (Retype text).

If it doesn't work

  • "No visible layers are painted here" appears: Hidden layers, areas hidden by a mask, and drawings at 0% opacity are not picked. Select the layer in the Layers panel, then turn off "Auto-select layer" and move it.
  • A different layer moves: When layers overlap, the topmost layer is picked. Select the layer you want to move and turn off "Auto-select layer".

Resize and rotate (Transform)

Shows a frame for resizing and rotating the artwork inside the selection, or the artwork of the whole layer. The frame appears both on the model in the 3D view and in the UV view.

  1. Select the area you want to change (not needed for the whole layer).
  2. Choose Edit → "Transform (on 3D/UV)" (Ctrl+T). A frame appears.
  3. Drag inside the frame to move, drag a corner to scale, and drag the circle on top to rotate.
  4. Press Enter to apply, or Esc to cancel.

Frame controls

ActionResult
Drag inside the frameMoves it
Drag a cornerScales it (hold Shift to change the aspect ratio)
Drag an edgeStretches or shrinks it in one direction only
Drag the circle on topRotates it (Shift for 15° steps)
Drag an edge or corner past the opposite sideFlips it
The bar below the frameFlip horizontal, flip vertical, opacity and the frame shape (Free transform and Warp)
  • You can grab either the 3D frame or the UV frame and it works the same way, with the other one following. If the artwork you're moving is visible in 3D, you can work in 3D alone without opening the UV view. If it isn't visible, the UV view opens, and closes when you finish.
  • Even over the 3D frame, you can move the view with right-drag, middle-drag, the wheel, or Space+drag. The frame follows when you change the view.
  • The 3D frame is placed to match the face at the center of the frame. When it crosses a UV seam, it matches the island in the middle.
  • When grid lines are shown in the UV view, the frame snaps to the grid squares (Grid and snapping).
  • The selection moves along with it.

Move the opposite side too (Mirror too)

When symmetry is on for drawing and the selection is on one side, "Mirror too" appears in the bar below the frame. If you leave it on while moving, the opposite side in 3D moves as a mirror image at the same time. The original artwork on the opposite side is replaced.

  • It works the same way in the Move/transform tool.
  • It doesn't appear when both sides are selected, when the selection crosses the center, or when there is no selection.
  • To turn symmetry on, see Paint symmetrically.

Add perspective and bend (Free transform and Warp)

The three icons in the bar below the frame switch the frame type. It works with Transform, the Move/transform tool, Add image, Paste, and Paste image onto 3D/UV (it doesn't appear on text frames).

Frame typeHow to use it
Scale/rotateDrag a corner to scale, the circle on top to rotate, and inside the frame to move (Frame controls)
Free transformDrag each of the four corners to reshape the frame. The artwork follows as if in perspective. A corner can't cross the opposite side, and the shape can't become concave
WarpDrag the grid points to bend the artwork. The grid is 3×3, and you can change it to 4×4 or 5×5 in the bar
  • In Free transform and Warp, you can also rotate by dragging the circle on top or just outside a corner (Shift for 15° steps). Drag inside the frame to move the whole thing.
  • Switching between Scale/rotate, Free transform and Warp keeps the current shape. When you go back to Scale/rotate, the current shape is painted into the artwork and you get a rectangular frame around it.
  • Flip horizontal, flip vertical and opacity also work. Applying counts as one Undo step (also when you moved a selection).
  • When pasting to 3D, the shape you bent on screen is projected onto the visible faces.

Copy, cut and paste

Copy or cut the artwork inside the selection (or the whole layer if there is no selection) and paste it. Pasted artwork goes into a new layer.

ActionKeyWhat it does
Edit → "Copy (inside selection)"Ctrl+CCopies the artwork on the selected layer
Edit → "Cut (inside selection)"Ctrl+XCopies it and erases the original
Edit → "Paste"Ctrl+VPastes it as a new layer at the same UV position. You can move it right away with the frame
Selection → "Copy selection to new layer" / "Cut selection to new layer"—Puts the artwork into a new layer at the same position immediately, without showing a frame
  • Even when several layers are selected, only one selected layer is affected.
  • If you press Ctrl+V on an image copied in another app, you can paste it and line up its frame (Add image).

If it doesn't work

  • You can't cut or clear: Layers that are locked, hidden, or have Lock transparency on are not cut or cleared (the reason appears below). Copying still works.
  • You can't make a new layer (Lite): Lite allows up to 4 layers per texture set, including the base (Editions).

Paste an image

Paste an image such as a logo or pattern, adjusting its size and position with a frame. There are three ways to paste.

How to pasteHow to startWhere the frame appears
Add imageFile → Import → "Import image as layer", drop an image onto the window, or press Ctrl+V on an image copied in another appOn the UV if the UV view is open; if it's closed, in the middle of the 3D view (projected onto the visible faces)
Paste image onto 3DEdit → "Paste image onto 3D"3D view (projected onto the visible faces)
Paste image onto UVEdit → "Paste image onto UV"UV view
  1. Drop an image onto the window. A frame for placing the image appears.
  2. Move the frame to adjust the size and position. You can also move the view while you adjust.
  3. Press Enter to apply. The image goes into a new layer named after the file.

The bar below the frame

  • The image is placed at its original aspect ratio, sized to fit the visible area. Transparent areas stay transparent.
  • Press Esc to cancel, and nothing is left behind. After you apply, one Undo removes it.
  • "Fill whole texture" (for Add image): stretches the image over the whole UV and puts it in a new layer. Use it for images drawn at the size of the UV.
  • "To new layer" (for pasting onto 3D; on by default): creates a new layer for each texture set you paste onto. Turn it off to paste into the current layer.
  • "Full strength on angled faces": pastes at full strength even on faces that turn sideways, instead of thinning out.
  • Flip horizontal, flip vertical, opacity and the frame shape (Free transform and Warp) are also available.
  • Symmetry and selections also apply.

Add text

Type text directly on the model and paste it. You can change the font, size and color for each character too.

  1. Select the "Text" tool (T). In the sub tools, choose "Horizontal text" or "Vertical".
  2. Click the 3D view (or UV view). A frame and the text window appear (you can also start from Edit → "Add text").
  3. Just start typing. Japanese input works too, and conversion candidates appear next to the characters.
  4. Press Ctrl+Enter to apply (Enter while typing adds a line break).

The text window

  • Set the font, size, color, bold, italic, vertical writing, line spacing, letter spacing, alignment and border.
  • While the text window is open, the foreground color in the Color panel becomes the text color and the background color becomes the border color. You can also pick colors by clicking the color squares in the window.
  • Turn on "Adjust with sliders" in the header to move size, border, line spacing and letter spacing with sliders too.
  • At the bottom of the font list, all the fonts installed on your PC are listed under "This PC's fonts".
  • With "Load font", you can also use a font file (.ttf or .otf), but only until you quit the app.
  • When the window overlaps the frame, you can move it by dragging its header ("Text"). It opens at the same position next time.
  • If you start by clicking the UV view, the text is pasted on the UV.

Controls while typing

ActionWhat it does
Click inside the frame (or double-click)Starts typing at that point
← → ↑ ↓, Home, EndMove the typing position (in vertical text, ↑ ↓ move between characters and ← moves to the next line)
Shift+arrow keys, drag inside the frameSelect characters
Backspace, DeleteDelete characters
Ctrl+dragMoves the frame (corners, edges and the circle on top work as usual)

While you're typing, key shortcuts (such as E for the Eraser) don't work. When you start, all the characters are selected, so whatever you type replaces them.

Change color or size per character

  • Select characters, then change the color (foreground or background), size, font, bold, italic, letter spacing or border, and only those characters change.
  • If you change something without selecting, it applies to the characters you type next. When you aren't typing (for example, after Esc), all the characters change.
  • The line "Color and size changes apply to:" in the text window shows which characters will change.
  • Line spacing, alignment and vertical writing affect all the characters. Characters of different sizes line up on a common bottom line.

Retype text (text layers)

Text you paste with "To new layer" turned on becomes a text layer, marked with a "T" in the layer list. Text pasted on the UV is always a text layer. You can change the characters, font, color and so on later and paste it again.

  1. In the layer list, double-click the text layer (or right-click → "Retype text").
  2. The text window and frame open in the state they were in when you pasted. The color and size of each character are kept too.
  3. Edit the text and press Ctrl+Enter to redraw it. Undo brings back the previous version.
  • You can also start by clicking the text with the Text tool, or with "Retype the selected text layer" in tool properties.
  • For text pasted on 3D, the view first returns to the one you had when you pasted, and then the frame appears. When you finish, the view goes back to what it was.
  • To rename it, right-click → "Rename".
  • Right-click → "Rasterize (text can no longer be edited)" turns it into an ordinary layer.
  • It is saved in the project file, so you can still retype it after reopening. In a PSD, it is written as an ordinary layer.

If it doesn't work

  • Pressing a key doesn't change the tool: Shortcuts don't work while you're typing text. Press Esc to stop typing.
  • The painting you added disappeared when you retyped: Once you paint over a text layer, its mark changes to "T+". Retyping discards what you added, so press Ctrl+Z to undo, move your added painting to a separate layer, and then retype.
  • "Font not found" appears: A loaded font can be used only until you quit the app. The text is retyped in another font. To get the original font back, load it again with "Load font".
  • You can't retype it: Text pasted into the current layer with "To new layer" turned off, and text that has been rasterized, are ordinary artwork.

Align lines with a ruler

Make your strokes snap to a ruler to draw straight lines or lines that converge on a center. It works in both the 3D view and the UV view.

  1. Select the "Ruler" tool and choose a ruler type in the sub tools.
  2. Create a ruler in the 3D view or UV view (see the table below for how).
  3. Switch to a pen or another tool and draw. Your lines follow the ruler.
Sub toolHow to create itLines it affects
Straight rulerDragA line you start near it (within the snap distance) becomes straight
Curve rulerClick to place points, then press Enter or double-click to finish (Esc to cancel)A line you start near it follows the curve
Concentric circle rulerThe point you click is the centerWherever you draw, the line becomes a circle at a constant distance from the center
Radial line rulerThe point you click is the centerWherever you draw, the line heads toward the center
Parallel line rulerDrag to set the directionWherever you draw, the line runs in that direction

Move, cut and remove rulers

  • Drag the circles on a ruler (ends, center, points) to move it. Set the snap distance in tool properties.
  • A ruler in the 3D view stays on screen when you move the view. You can also place a separate ruler in the UV view.
  • While a ruler exists, a strip appears at the bottom center of that pane (ruler type, "Snap" on/off, and "Delete"). "Delete" removes only the ruler in that pane.
  • Toggle snapping with Display → Lines & rulers → "Snap to ruler" (Ctrl+Alt+R). Remove the ruler with "Remove ruler" in the same place.
  • While you're placing the points of a curve ruler, Ctrl+Z undoes the last point. Redo it with Ctrl+Y or Ctrl+Shift+Z.

If it doesn't work

  • Lines go straight or curve by themselves: A ruler is still there. Turn off "Snap" in the strip at the bottom, or remove it with "Delete" (Ctrl+Alt+R also turns it off).
  • Lines don't follow the straight or curve ruler: It only affects lines you start drawing near the ruler. Increase the snap distance in tool properties.
