Layers

Layers are stacked for each texture set. If you keep the base coat, shadows and patterns on separate layers, you can adjust their colors and strength later. This page also covers the features you apply to layers, such as blending, masks and filters.

Screenshots show the Japanese interface. Menu names in the text follow the selected app language.

Start with this
  1. Click "Add" (Ctrl+Shift+N) in the Layers panel to add a new layer (details).
  2. Click a row to select a layer and draw on it. The eye icon shows or hides it, and Opacity changes its strength (details).
  3. For shadows, turn on "Clipping" for a layer above the base coat and set its blend mode to "Multiply". Your paint then stays inside the base coat (details).
  4. To hide part of a layer without erasing it, use a layer mask (details).
  5. If you make a mistake, press Ctrl+Z. The History panel lets you go back several steps at once (details).

Add layers to separate your painting

What you draw goes into the selected layer. If you split layers by part, it's easier to delete them or change their colors later.

  1. Click "Add" at the bottom of the Layers panel. A new layer is created above the selected layer.
  2. Click the row of the layer you want to draw on to select it.
  3. Draw.
  4. Click the eye icon to show or hide the layer.
  5. Double-click the name to rename the layer (press Enter to confirm).
In Lite, each texture set can have up to 4 layers, including the base (drawing layers and adjustment layers count; folders and masks don't). At 4 layers, "Layers 4 / 4 (incl. base, Lite)" appears below the panel, and "Add" and "Duplicate" get a lock icon.

  • A layer with a stroke gets the stroke baked into pixels before it's merged.
  • If you merge an adjustment layer down, its correction is baked into the pixels of the layer below.
  • Pro In a normal map folder, you can merge the visible layers running up from the bottom. Set the folder's opacity to 100% and disable the folder's mask first (Paint target).

Select several at once

  • Ctrl+click names to add or remove layers one at a time, and Shift+click to select everything in between (Ctrl+click on a thumbnail makes a selection, which is a different thing).
  • While several are selected, visibility, blend mode, opacity, clipping, lock transparency, lock, color tag, delete, duplicate, put in folder (Ctrl+Shift+G) and merge apply to all of them at once. Undo is a single step.
  • Drag any one of the selected layers and all of them move together, keeping their order. Release in the middle of a folder row and they all go into the folder.
  • "Move up one" and "Move down one" work on one layer at a time. Copy and cut also work on only the one active layer.

Select with keys or from 3D

  • Press Alt+] to select the layer above and Alt+[ to select the layer below.
  • Right-click in the 3D view or UV view → "Select layer painted here" lists the visible layers painted at that spot. The top one is marked. Layers you can't see, such as areas hidden by a mask or layers at 0% opacity, aren't listed.
  • You can also select with "Auto-select layer" of the Move/transform tool (Move/transform tool).
  • If a row selected this way isn't visible, the list scrolls until you can see it (a closed folder opens). To turn this off, uncheck "Scroll the list until the layer selected with keys, etc. is visible" in the … (More) menu or in the list's right-click menu.

If it doesn't work

  • You can't add a layer (Lite): Lite allows up to 4 layers per texture set, including the base. Delete or merge a layer and you can add one again (Editions).
  • Painting leaves no color: Check whether the selected layer is hidden or locked, and whether the draw-on target is set to the mask. The paint may also be hidden behind a layer above.
  • You drew on the wrong layer: Undo with Ctrl+Z, then choose the layer you want again with right-click in 3D → "Select layer painted here".

Overlay shadows and light (blend mode and opacity)

The blend mode is how a layer mixes with the picture below it. "Multiply" is commonly used for shadows, and "Screen" or "Add (glow)" for light.

  1. Select the layer you'll draw shadows or light on.
  2. Choose a blend mode in the blend mode field at the top of the panel. You can also hover over the field and turn the wheel to switch to the previous or next blend mode (consecutive turns are undone with one Ctrl＋Z).
  3. Adjust the strength with Opacity.

List of blend modes

There are 28 in all. In the field, they're arranged in the following groups.

Folders have one more, "Pass through" (the default) (Folders).

Add (glow) and Color dodge (glow)

  • Unlike the normal Add and Color dodge, they glow strongly even where you've lowered the opacity or at the soft edges of a soft brush.
  • Add (glow) keeps adding until the light saturates, and Color dodge (glow) glows more strongly the brighter the area is. Even over transparent areas, they glow in the layer's color.
  • You can choose them as the blend mode of layers, folders and adjustment layers, for stroke blending, and for a brush's drawing mode.
  • In a PSD, they're written as a close blend mode (Linear dodge or Color dodge) with "[glow]" added after the layer name. When PetaNuri3D imports the PSD, they become glow again. In other apps, semi-transparent areas look different.

Paint without spilling over (clipping, lock transparency, lock)

When you paint shadows or patterns over a base coat, this keeps them from going outside the painted area. Each one is turned on or off with the icons at the top of the panel.

FeatureWhat it doesOther ways to switch it
Clipping (↳)Shows this layer only where the layer just below it is painted (the layer below is the foundation)Ctrl+Alt+G; right-click → "Clip to layer below"
Lock transparencyTransparent areas stay transparent when you draw (only the color changes)Layer → Clipping & locks → "Toggle lock transparency"
LockPrevents drawing on the layerLayer → Clipping & locks → "Toggle lock"
  1. Select the base coat layer and click "Add" to add a layer above it.
  2. Click the clipping (↳) icon at the top of the panel.
  3. Set the blend mode to "Multiply" and paint the shadow. It doesn't go outside the base coat.
The clipped drawing is still there, so if you enlarge the base coat, it shows up in the new area too. Lock transparency protects the shape of the layer's own drawing.

Group into folders

Putting related layers in a folder shortens the list and lets you show or hide them, or change their opacity, all at once.

  1. Select the layers you want to group (any number).
  2. Hold Shift and click the "Folder" button (Ctrl+Shift+G). The selected layers go into the folder.
  • Clicking just the "Folder" button (Ctrl+G) makes an empty folder.
  • Drag a layer row onto the middle of a folder row to put it inside. The upper half or lower half puts it above or below the folder.
  • The blend mode "Pass through" (the default) layers the contents directly onto the picture outside the folder. With other modes, the contents are combined into one layer first and then layered.
  • To dissolve a folder, use … (More) or right-click → "Ungroup folder". The contents move out of it.
  • Folders can have masks and strokes too.

Hide part of a layer (layer mask)

A mask hides part of a layer's picture without erasing it. You can bring the hidden part back at any time.

  1. Select the layer you want to hide part of.
  2. Click the "Mask" button → "Add reveal-all mask". A mask thumbnail appears on the row.
  3. Click the mask thumbnail. The draw-on target becomes the mask.
  4. Draw in black to hide and in white to show. Gray is half visible.
  5. Click the pixel thumbnail to return the draw-on target to the picture.

Tips for working with masks

  • Shift+click the mask thumbnail to switch the mask on or off (a red diagonal line shows while it's off).
  • Alt+click the mask thumbnail to show the mask in black and white.
  • Drag the mask thumbnail onto another layer's row to move it, or Alt+drag to copy it. An adjustment layer's mask is always copied.
  • If the destination already has a mask, it's replaced. If the sizes differ, the mask is stretched to fit.
  • Fills of a selection, and fills with Alt/Ctrl+Backspace, also go into a mask according to the brightness of the color.
  • You can assign keys to mask operations in the key bindings in Preferences.

If it doesn't work

  • Painting doesn't change the picture: The draw-on target is still the mask. Click the pixel thumbnail to return to the picture.
  • The mask has no effect: The mask is disabled (a red diagonal line). Shift+click the mask thumbnail to enable it.
  • Painting on the mask doesn't change how it looks: Drawing in white means "show". To hide, draw in black or use the Eraser.

Make the list easier to read (color tags and thumbnails)

When you have many layers, make them easier to find by sorting them with color tags or enlarging the thumbnails.

  1. Select the layers you want to color (any number).
  2. In the row with the clipping and lock icons, click the colored circle and ▾ box at the far left (or right-click → "Color tag").
  3. Choose from none, red, orange, yellow, green, cyan, blue, purple and gray. A thick colored band appears at the left of the row.
  • A folder's color continues as a thin line at the left of the layers inside it. Undo is a single step.
  • When there are color tags, "Show by color: All" and circles for the colors in use appear above the list. Click a circle to list only the layers of that color (the picture doesn't change). Click it again, or click "All", to go back.
  • Color tags stay in the project file. With PSD export and import Standard, they become the layer colors in the PSD (cyan becomes blue).

List size and order

  • In … (More) or the right-click "Thumbnail size", you can choose Small (24px), Medium (40px, the default), Large (64px) or Extra large (96px).
  • With "Put the button row on top" in … (More), you can move the row of buttons from the bottom to above the list.
  • If you turn off "Move layers and sub tools by dragging with the pen" in … (More), dragging with the pen scrolls the list. Space + drag always scrolls.
  • Drag the panel's border downward to make the list taller.
  • All of these stay the same the next time you launch.

Tile a repeating pattern

Repeats an image or pattern up, down, left and right to fill a layer. Use it for clothing fabric patterns, for example.

  1. Select the pixel layer you want to tile. The current drawing is replaced by the pattern.
  2. Under "Repeating pattern" in the layer properties, click "Choose a pattern to fill". The pattern list opens.
  3. Choose from "Image file…", "Reference image" or the pattern pictures listed below them. A pattern becomes a design painted in the foreground color.
  4. Adjust the arrangement with the items in the table below.

Arrangement and color

  • The top row shows the current pattern picture and name. Click it to open the pattern list.
  • You can decide where the pattern shows with a mask.
  • The arrangement and pattern stay in the project file. You can undo changes.

If it doesn't work

  • What you added later disappeared: What you painted after tiling is lost when you change the arrangement (a confirmation appears first). Press Ctrl+Z to undo.
  • The pattern doesn't connect at a seam: At seams where the islands on the two sides differ in direction or shape, fitting won't make the pattern connect.
  • You can't tile: You can't tile a locked layer, a layer with lock transparency, a hidden layer, a text layer or a normal map image layer.

Copy one side to the other (Mirror copy left/right)

Copies paint you forgot to symmetrize to the opposite side of the 3D model. Even if the UVs differ between left and right, it finds the matching spot by shape and copies it there.

  1. Select the layer you want to copy.
  2. Choose Layer → "Mirror copy left/right" (it's also in the right-click menu of a layer row).
  3. Click "Left side → Right side" or "Right side → Left side". The colors on the destination side are replaced.
  • Left and right are as seen from the model (the reverse of what you see from the front). The center is the same as the center of "Paint symmetrically" (Paint symmetrically).
  • Turn on "Copy to a new layer (only the copied area)" and only the copied area goes into a new layer.
  • When there's a selection, choose "Copy selection to opposite side" or "Copy into selection" (replaces the inside of the selection with the colors from the opposite side).
  • For masks, use "Mirror copy mask left/right" in the Mask menu.
  • Areas with no left/right counterpart (things that exist on one side only) stay as they are. One Ctrl+Z undoes it.

Add a border (Stroke) Standard

Adds a border when layers are stacked, without changing the layer's drawing itself. You can change the thickness and color later, or turn it off.

  1. Select the layer you want to add a border to.
  2. Click "Add" in the layer properties. The Stroke window appears.
  3. Set the size, position, color and so on. The look changes right away as you move the values.
  4. Confirm with OK (Enter). Cancel (Esc) puts everything back as it was.

SizeThickness (in texture px)

Blend mode, Opacity, ColorHow the border is layered, its strength and its color

Stroke menu

It's under Layer → Stroke, or right-click a layer → "Stroke". A layer with a stroke shows an "fx" mark on its row. Click it to open the settings window.

  • "Stroke settings": Opens the window.
  • "Show stroke": Shows or hides the stroke while keeping its values (the same as "Display" in the layer properties).
  • "Copy stroke" and "Paste stroke": Copy the stroke to other layers. If you've selected several layers, it's pasted onto all of them.
  • "Clear stroke": Removes the stroke.
  • "Bake stroke into pixels": Makes the picture include the stroke and removes the stroke itself (for a folder, the folder is merged into one layer together with its stroke).

Resulting shape and saving

  • The border follows the shape after the mask is applied. A clipped layer stays within the painted area of its base.
  • You can add it to folders (the combined shape of the contents) and text layers (it stays even if you retype the text).
  • Merge down and Merge folder bake the stroke before merging.
  • It's stored in the project file along with its values. PNG and material exports use the picture with the stroke, and in a PSD it becomes a layer with the stroke baked into pixels (PSD).
  • Other effects added in an earlier version or in a PSD (such as drop shadow) open looking exactly as before. You can't change them, but you can use "Bake into pixels" or "Remove" in the layer properties.
Adding, changing and pasting strokes requires Standard. Even in Lite, the strokes in project files you open look as they did, and you can show, hide, copy, clear and bake them into pixels.

If it doesn't work

  • The border is cut off at an island edge or spills onto the next island: Strokes are calculated on the UV. Parts that go past the island edge may look cut off at the island edge in 3D. On export, the parts outside the island are handled the same way as padding.

Change colors later (adjustment layers) Standard

Stacks a brightness or color correction over the picture below without rewriting it. You can change the values any time.

  1. Select the layer just above the picture you want to correct.
  2. Click the "Adjust" button in the panel and choose a type. An adjustment layer is created.
  3. Move the values in the Layer properties panel. While you drag, only the picture changes, and when you release, it counts as one step you can undo.

There are 15 types: Hue/Saturation/Lightness, Brightness/Contrast, Levels, Invert, Grayscale, Posterize, Threshold, Gradation map, Curves, Color Balance, Exposure, Color Temperature, Vibrance, Replace Color and Add noise. The values are the same as for Filters.

  • An adjustment layer applies to everything visible below it.
  • You can limit where it applies with a mask. When you select an adjustment layer, the draw-on target is the mask. "Create mask from selection" can replace the current mask.
  • With clipping on, it applies only to the layer just below it.
  • "Merge with layer below" bakes the correction into the pixels of the layer below.
  • In a PSD, Curves, Color Balance, Vibrance, Exposure and Gradation map are exported and imported as adjustment layers.
  • "Solid color" in the Adjust button's list creates a layer of one color. You can change the color in Layer properties. In a PSD, it is also read and written as a solid color layer.
  • In Lite, you can see the values of adjustment layers in project files you open, but you can't change them (a lock icon appears).
Features that read the surrounding picture, such as Blur, Sharpen and Mosaic, don't become adjustment layers (they would be too heavy). Apply them as filters.

Apply a filter Standard

Applies a color correction or blur to the picture of the selected layer itself. Unlike an adjustment layer, the picture is rewritten (one Ctrl+Z undoes it).

  1. Select the layer you want to filter.
  2. From the "Filters" menu, choose a group and then a filter name. A small window appears.
  3. Move the values. The model's look changes on the spot.
  4. Confirm with OK (Enter). To cancel, use Cancel (Esc).

Island edges and transparent areas

  • Blur and sharpen are calculated only inside UV islands, so island edges don't get darker or fainter. To blur across seams, use the Blend tool (Blur, Smudge and Blend).
  • Mosaic also averages opacity for each cell, and Sharpen also sharpens opacity. Reduce Noise (Median) also removes dots on top of transparent areas.
  • Extract Lines, Emboss and High Pass read brightness by treating transparent areas as white paper. The lines from Extract Lines and the result of High Pass also appear on areas that were transparent.
  • On a layer with lock transparency, opacity stays as it was.

If it doesn't work

  • A filter changes nothing: It isn't applied to hidden or locked layers. It also changes nothing if the selection exists only in other texture sets. Check "Target" and "Not applied:" in the window.
  • The menu items have a lock icon (Lite): Filters need Standard. Convert luminance to opacity and Create padding on a new layer work in Lite too.

Replace colors with a gradation map Standard

Replaces dark to bright areas with the colors from the left to the right of a gradient. It's available both as a filter (Tone) and as an adjustment layer.

  1. Choose Filters → Tone → "Gradation map" (for an adjustment layer, use the "Adjust" button). At first it has two colors, foreground color → background color.
  2. Choose a preset, or add color stops and set their colors.
  3. Confirm with OK (Enter).

Presets

  • There are 240. Choose from the groups (Basics / mono, Sunset / sunrise, Pastel, Neon, Metal, Skin / people, Seasons, Photographic toning, Illustration / anime coloring and more), or search by name.

Make white transparent (Convert luminance to opacity)

Makes white areas transparent and keeps dark areas as black shadow. Use this when you want to recolor only the AO shadow, for example. It's available from Lite.

  1. Select a drawing layer other than the base, such as the AO layer. If lock or lock transparency is on, turn it off.
  2. Choose Filters → "Convert luminance to opacity". White becomes transparent, and darker areas become more opaque black.
  3. To change the shadow color, turn on lock transparency and paint with the color you like. The light and dark of the shadow stays as transparency.
This rewrites the pixels of the selected layer (undo with Ctrl+Z). It can't be used on the base, masks or adjustment layers. It isn't a way to make areas transparent while keeping their original colors.

Make a layer that extends color outside islands (Create padding on a new layer)

Puts only the part where the colors at the edges of the islands in the current picture are extended outside the islands into a new layer. Use it when you want to see the export padding as a layer or repaint it. It's available in every edition.

  1. Choose Filters → "Create padding on a new layer".
  2. Set "Width (px)" from 1 to 32.
  3. Click "Create". A layer with a name like "Padding (8px)" is created right above the selected layer.
  • It's made from the color of all the layers stacked together. The inside of the islands is transparent.
  • One Ctrl+Z undoes it. It can't be made in the 2D canvas.
  • For the padding used on export, see Export bleed (padding).

Make base colors from line art (Auto fill)

Fills each area enclosed by line art according to the color hints you place. Use it to make a base coat quickly.

  1. Choose Layer → "Auto fill (fill regions from color hints)". A window appears at the upper left of the 3D view.
  2. Choose the line art layer in "Line art".
  3. Click "Create hint layer". A new layer for hints is selected.
  4. On the hint layer, roughly place the colors you want in each area.
  5. Click "Run". An "Auto fill" layer is created right below the line art (it paints under the lines too).

If it doesn't work

  • Color leaks into the next area: A line has a break. Draw the lines so that they close on the visible faces (if a line runs off the body and breaks, color leaks from there). Raising "Close gaps" also helps prevent it.
  • Some areas aren't painted: Areas without a hint aren't painted. Place a hint, or turn on "Also fill areas without hints with the nearest color".

Undo and history

You can undo mistakes with Undo (Ctrl+Z). With the History panel, you can go back several steps at once.

To do thisHow
Undo one stepUndo (Ctrl+Z; hold it down to keep undoing)
Redo what you undidRedo (Ctrl+Y or Ctrl+Shift+Z)
Go back or forward several stepsClick a row in the "History" panel. You go back or forward at once to the state after that operation
  • History lists the operations so far, oldest first. ▶ marks the current position, and dimmed rows are operations you can redo.
  • Up to 30 steps are recorded, and the oldest are dropped first.
  • Show or hide the panel from Display → Windows.

Operations that can't be undone

Operations that change the picture (layers, masks, paint targets, material settings) can be undone. Operations that change only how things look, and app settings, are not recorded in Undo.

  • Not recorded: turning a texture set's visibility on or off, Visible area (hide, show selected only, show all), object visibility, blend shapes, the view, shading, the mesh wireframe, adding and reordering color sets, sub tools, settings and key bindings. A layer's visibility is part of the picture, so it can be undone.
  • "Hide selected" (H) records only the part of the selection that gets deselected, as one step. To bring back hidden areas, use "Show all" (Alt+H) (Visible area). Right after hiding, the first Ctrl+Z only gives you a notice, and pressing it again undoes the painting before that.
  • Deleting a color set swatch can be undone.
These operations can't be undone, so a confirmation appears first: deleting a sub tool, deleting a color set, resetting key bindings, and resetting the brush size list. Changing the texture resolution clears the whole Undo history.

Rest layers (save memory)

So that stacking lots of 4096-resolution layers doesn't make things slow, layers you haven't touched for a while are taken out of GPU memory and kept elsewhere. The look, export and saving are unchanged.

  • The GPU holds only the selected layer, the combined picture of everything below it, and the combined picture of everything above it. Other layers are shrunk and kept one at a time while you're not drawing on them (areas you haven't painted aren't kept).
  • Selecting, drawing on, undoing, merging or exporting a resting layer brings it back on the spot. With selections that reload many layers, "Preparing…" appears and then it switches (about 0.1 to 0.3 seconds for 80 layers at 4096).
  • If there are layers above with a blend mode other than Normal, or adjustment layers, those stay in the GPU.
  • Layers also stay resting when you open a project file.
  • To turn it off, use File → Preferences → Painting → Performance, "Rest layers you aren't drawing on" (on by default). When it's off, all layers are kept in the GPU.
There's also a mechanism that rests whole texture sets you haven't selected (Texture set memory savings).
