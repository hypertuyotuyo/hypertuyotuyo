Final look and lilToon materials Pro

Paint while you see the same calculated look as lilToon (shadows, MatCap, rim light, outlines and more). You can also paint shadow color and masks, and hand everything to Unity as a lilToon material together with its settings.

Screenshots show the Japanese interface. Menu names in the text follow the selected app language.

This is a Pro feature. In Lite, menus and icons show a lock icon, and the "Material" panel shows only a description. In Standard, the entry points don't appear (Edition comparison). "Final look" is a beta, so it can look slightly different from how it appears in Unity.

Start with this
  1. Set Shading to "Final look" with the icons at the top left of the 3D view, and you can paint with the lilToon look (details).
  2. Change settings such as shadows, rim light and outlines in the "Material" panel on the right (details).
  3. To paint shadow color or masks, choose the paint target with the "Paint:" buttons in the panel. When you are done, click "Back to color" (details).
  4. To bring in an existing lilToon material along with its settings, use File → Import → Load lilToon material (details).
  5. To hand it to Unity, use File → Export → Export materials to make a .unitypackage (details).

Check with the lilToon look while painting

When you set Shading to "Final look", the colors you paint appear with the same shadow and light calculations as lilToon. You can paint while seeing the final look before you take it to Unity.

  1. Of the five Shading icons at the top left of the 3D view, click the second from the right (Final look).
  2. Paint as usual. Shadows, rim light, outlines and so on appear on top of the colors you paint.
  3. To check only the painted colors, switch back to the leftmost one, "Unlit (painted colors)".
  • Other entry points: Display → Shading → "Final look (lilToon/MToon, beta)", the 3D view's right-click → Display, and "Show final look (lilToon, beta)" in the "Material" panel.
  • What it shows: shadows (Shadow color 1, Shadow color 2, Shadow color 3, border color), normal map, MatCap, MatCap 2nd, rim light, backlight, rim shade, emission, outline, reflection settings, glitter, anisotropic reflection, dither, dissolve, parallax, AudioLink and color adjustment.

Change settings in the "Material" panel

Settings are per material. Item names follow the names on lilToon's settings screen. Every change can be undone.

Change the light direction

  • Change it with "Light horizontal angle", "Light height" and "Light intensity" under "Display and lighting" in the "Material" panel. You can also change it from the ▼ next to the Shading icons (Light direction).
  • If you turn on "Light follows camera", the light hits the model the same way when you rotate the view, which makes painting easier. If you turn it off, the light is fixed to the model.
  • This light is for checking the look. The light of an exported material is decided by the lights you place in Unity.

Check with the MToon look

You can paint a VRM model while checking its MToon look (a display preview, beta).

  1. In the "Material" panel under "Display and lighting", click "MToon".
  2. Click "Show final look (MToon, beta)".
  3. Paint while adjusting the MToon color, shadow position, sharpness, ambient light, rim, emission and outline.
  • If a VRM0 or VRM1 model has MToon settings, you start from its color, shadow, rim, emission and outline and its supporting images. A model without them starts from the standard look.
  • "Reset to original MToon settings" resets only the look adjustments. The layers you painted are not deleted. Click "lilToon" to return to the lilToon look.
  • Adjustments can be undone and redone, and they are saved in the project file.
MToon is a display for checking only. PNG and PSD are exported with the colors as painted, and the material export stays for lilToon. MToon materials and VRM are not exported.

If it doesn't work

  • The "Final look" icon has a lock, or there is no icon: This is a Pro feature. Lite shows a lock, and Standard doesn't show it.
  • It looks a little different from Unity: It is a beta, so differences in light or background can cause differences. The light is for checking only, and in Unity the lights you place decide the look.
  • It went back to the normal display when I chose MToon: UV settings that change the position, scale or rotation of the main image, and images that use a UV other than UV0, are not supported. The reason is shown when it switches back. UV animation is shown stopped.
  • The area I want transparent isn't cut out: Set the rendering mode under "Basic" to "Cutout" or "Transparent". "Opaque" ignores transparency.

Paint shadow color, masks and bump (paint targets)

You can paint not only the main color but also images for shadow color, shadow mask, normal map (bump), emission and more, with your usual pens. Each paint target gets its own folder in the Layers panel.

  1. In the "Material" panel, click a "Paint:" button in a section and choose the paint target (you can also choose it from right-click → Paint target).
  2. A folder for that paint target (for example "Shadow color 1") is created above Main color in the Layers panel, and a layer inside it is selected.
  3. Paint with your usual pen, brush, fill, gradient, blur and filters.
  4. Click "Back to color" at the top center of the 3D view to return to normal color painting. Clicking a layer outside the folder also takes you back.
Paint targetHow it works
Shadow color 1, Shadow color 2Shadows in the painted area take that color (reddish skin, bluish hair shadows and so on). Where you haven't painted, the shadow is the original color multiplied by the shadow color in the settings
Shadow maskWhite = shadows applied; black = no shadows
Shadow AOThe darker it is, the more readily shadows appear (around the neck, inside the hair, where clothing overlaps). With Bake AO, you can also put the result here
Normal mapYou paint it by height. White raises, black carves, and gray is flat (for clothing wrinkles, seams and hair strands. Paint a normal map)
MatCap mask, MatCap 2nd mask, Outline widthWhite = applied; black = not applied
Rim light colorMultiplies by the painted color (black removes it)
Emission textureOnly the painted area glows, in the painted color (multiplied by the "Emission color" in the settings)

Other paint targets

Right-click → Paint target → Other masks (shadow color 3, glitter, etc.) lists more. You can paint them while that feature is in use.

  • Shadow: Shadow color 3, Shadow blur mask (the more you paint, the softer the edge of the shadow)
  • Color and transparency: Color adjustment mask (adjusts only the painted area), Alpha mask (paint how transparent it is)
  • Emission: Emission mask, Emission 2nd texture, Emission 2nd mask
  • Edge and outline: Backlight color, Rim shade mask, Outline color
  • Material look: Normal map 2nd mask, Glitter color (black hides the glitter), Smoothness, Metallic (white = as set, black = 0), Reflection color, Anisotropy mask, Anisotropy noise
  • More: Dissolve mask, Dissolve noise, Parallax height (white = near, black = far), AudioLink mask

  • Inside the folder it works like ordinary layers: you can add, delete and reorder layers, and use opacity, blend mode, mask, clipping, Stroke and Merge down. The image made by stacking the layers inside is the image for that paint target.
  • Only the paint targets in use get folders. You can't move or delete the folder itself (to remove it, use "Clear").
  • Layers inside the folder don't count toward Lite's layer limit. Areas you erase with the eraser return to the unpainted state.
  • The UV pane also shows the image of the paint target you are painting.

Paint a normal map (bump)

  1. In the "Material" panel under "Normal map", turn on "Use normal map".
  2. Click "Normal map" under "Paint:". A "Normal map" folder is created at the top of the layers.
  3. Raise with white and carve with black. Blur softens it. You can change the strength later with "Normal map strength".
  • Normal map images made in other software: Use "Add normal map image", or select a layer inside the folder and drop the image onto the window. It goes in as normals, not as height (the layer gets a "Normal" badge). For an image with DirectX orientation, turn on "DirectX orientation (green flipped)" in Layer properties.
  • Add texture from a pattern: Lay down patterns such as knit, denim, canvas and herringbone as repeating bump. Change the size, rotation and seams with "Repeating pattern" in Layer properties. You can decide where it applies with a mask.
  • While a layer inside the folder is selected, the UV pane shows the normal map's color (blue-violet) instead of the height (gray). To switch back to gray, use right-click → Display → "Show the normal map folder as height (gray) (UV)".
  • There is also a tool for drawing bump (Bump).
  • When you export, PNG gives a single <name>_normal.png, and PSD keeps the layers separate inside a "Normal map" group.

If it doesn't work

  • "Can't add any more" appears: A single material can use at most 16 textures at once in the Final look display. Turn off other features, or "Clear" paint targets you don't use, and then you can use it.
  • I pick a color but can only paint gray: You are painting a paint target that reads only brightness. To go back to normal colors, click "Back to color".
  • I can't delete or move a paint target folder: Folders can't be deleted. To remove a whole paint target, click "Clear" at the top center.

Layer Main color 2nd and 3rd

You can paint the images for lilToon's "Main color 2nd" and "Main color 3rd" separately from the main image. Use it when you want to layer a design or pattern from another image.

  1. In the "Material" panel under "Main color 2nd", turn on "Use Main color 2nd".
  2. A "Main color 2nd" folder is created at the top of the Layers panel.
  3. Select the folder, then add layers and paint. You can also drag ordinary layers into it.
  4. Set how it overlaps (color, opacity, blend mode, apply light brightness) in the panel.
  • Only the layers inside the folder become the 2nd (3rd) texture, and they don't mix into the main image. The blend modes are Normal, Add, Screen and Multiply.
  • With the paint target "Main color 2nd mask", you can paint where it overlaps (white) and where it doesn't (black).
  • The folder can't be moved, deleted or duplicated. While it contains layers, you can't turn off "On" (if you empty it, you can turn it off, and the folder goes away too).
  • On export, they become separate images (<name>_main2nd.png, <name>_main3rd.png, and the masks you painted). In PSD they stay as a folder, and when you import it again it returns to this folder. In the material, they go into lilToon's "Main color 2nd/3rd".

Change settings for only some objects (split materials)

You can change settings such as shadow color, MatCap, rim light and outline for each object while keeping the same texture (the image you paint). For example, with the hair texture unchanged, you can make only the animal ears' shadows red.

  1. In the 3D view, right-click the object, or right-click its row in the Objects panel.
  2. Choose "Give only this object a separate material". A copy of the current material is added under the object's name and assigned to it.
  3. In the "Material" panel, change the settings of that material.
  • At first, each texture set has one material with the same name. If you don't split, nothing changes.
  • With "Assign material" in the right-click menu, or "Assign to object" in the panel, you can choose which material to use. You can choose only materials that use the same texture set.
  • In the list at the top of the panel, you can add (a copy of the current material), rename, delete, and move up or down. The texture set's original material can't be deleted.
  • The order of the list is the priority. When objects overlap at the same spot, the upper material is chosen.
  • The panel switches to the material of the spot you touch in 3D, and the status bar below shows "Material: name (texture set name)".
  • On export, there is one .mat per material. The textures are one set per texture set, and the materials point to the same ones.

Load a lilToon material

You can load a lilToon material that comes with an avatar and keep painting from its settings and images. When you export, only the parts you repainted are written back into the original material.

  1. Choose File → Import → "Load lilToon material (.mat, .unitypackage)".
  2. Choose a .mat, or a .unitypackage that contains one. You can also drop it onto the window.
  3. If you chose several, check the texture sets they will go into in the window that appears (they go into the set with the matching name).
  4. If any textures can't be found, they are listed so you can choose the images (if you don't, they are loaded without them).
  • You can also choose it under "Start with" on the New screen (Types of starting point).
  • Settings that come in: rendering mode, render face, Main color, shadow, normal map, MatCap/2nd, rim light, backlight, rim shade, emission, outline, Main color 2nd/3rd and more.
  • Images that come in: Main color goes to the base layer (every layer, if it is a PSD); 2nd/3rd go to their folders; shadow colors, masks, rim light color, emission, outline width and AO go to paint targets; MatCap goes to MatCap; and the normal map goes to the Normal map folder (as normals).
  • Textures are searched for in the project (Assets, Packages) that contains the .mat. The first time takes several seconds to over ten seconds. For a .unitypackage, it searches inside the package.
  • If you choose several .mat files that use the same texture, they go into the same texture set, and from the second one on they become separate materials.
  • Loading can be undone (one step). That set's material settings, paint targets, base image and Main color 2nd/3rd are replaced.

Unreproduced settings

Settings that PetaNuri3D doesn't imitate (stencil, distance fade, UV animation, texture tiling and so on) are listed in "Unreproduced settings" after you load. The values stay as they are and go back unchanged when you export. You can also see the list from the "lilToon: (name).mat" section of the panel.

Write back to the original material

  • When you export a loaded material, only the lines for the settings you changed and the textures you repainted are rewritten, in the same place and with the same ID as the original .mat. Textures you didn't change are not exported.
  • If you import the .unitypackage into the project, the original materials and textures are overwritten in place (assignments to the model stay).
  • If you repaint a texture that was not a PNG or was not found, a new PNG is created next to the .mat and swapped in.
  • To export as a new material, click "Unlink from source" in the panel.
This overwrites files in the original project. If you don't want to rewrite the materials of an avatar you bought directly, copy the project first, or click "Unlink from source" before you export.

Export materials and hand them to Unity

Exports the lilToon materials (.mat) together with the images you painted. When you import it into Unity, you get materials with the textures already assigned.

  1. Choose File → Export → "Export materials (.unitypackage / folder)" (or "Export materials" in the "Material" panel).
  2. Check "Materials to export".
  3. Set "Destination folder name (under Assets)", the resolution and "Padding".
  4. Click "Export to .unitypackage" and choose where to save.
  5. In Unity, double-click the resulting .unitypackage, or drag it into the Project window, to import it.
  • What goes in: materials (.mat) and images such as Main color, shadow color, masks, normal map and MatCap. Paint targets are included only while that feature is in use.
  • "Export to folder": If you choose the Assets folder of a Unity project, it writes directly with .meta files (app (exe) only).
  • Images have their color spread outside the islands by the width of "Padding" (export bleed).
  • If you export the same work again with the same folder name, what you imported before is overwritten in place (assignments to the model stay).
  • Sets with a ! (sets that aren't in the model) are not exported.
What is exported is a material for lilToon (2.3 series). Make sure lilToon is installed in the project you import into.

If it doesn't work

  • The material turns pink in Unity: lilToon isn't installed in that project. Installing lilToon fixes it.
  • "Export to folder" isn't available: It works only in the app (exe). Export to .unitypackage instead.
  • It doesn't show on the avatar: Assign the exported material in the model's material slots. If you loaded an original .mat and exported it, the original material is overwritten in place.
