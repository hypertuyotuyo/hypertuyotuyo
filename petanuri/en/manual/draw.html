Draw

Draw directly on the 3D view with a pen. Select a tool in the left toolbar, then adjust its feel with Sub tool and Tool properties.

Screenshots show the Japanese interface. Menu names in the text follow the selected app language.

Lite, Standard, Pro, and the Pro upgrade are available on BOOTH.

Notes beginning with “v1.2.0:” on this page are a release preview. The currently available version is v1.1.2.

Try drawing first

  1. Starting a stroke on the model automatically switches to that texture set (such as body) before painting. You can also choose it in the Texture sets panel on the right.
  2. Select Pen (P) in the left toolbar, then choose a sub tool such as G-pen.
  3. Pick a color in the color panel and draw on the model in 3D.
  4. Rotate by right-dragging (pen side button) or Space + dragging; zoom with the wheel.
On the model, Alt + right-click also selects that set. See Texture set.

It respects selections and Lock transparency and works with symmetry. Hand: while selected, left-drag or pen-drag moves the view (hold Ctrl in 3D to zoom).

Also paint back and hidden faces

Just below opacity in Tool properties, “Also paint back and hidden faces” (eraser: “Erase back and hidden faces”) paints inside a sphere centered on the visible face under the tip, matching the brush circle's apparent size. It reaches back/inner faces hidden from the camera, painting inside hair, layered clothes and between fingers at once.

  • For pens, brushes, brush collections, erasers and gradients, this is enabled by default. Turn it off to paint only visible faces.
  • Also available for decorations, patterns and surface relief (off by default). Blur, smudge, blend and stamps use displayed colors/images and affect only visible faces.
  • Hardness softens the edge. Image tips (pencil, chalk, spray and many brush-collection presets) work too. “Surface-aligned” follows face direction; “Screen-aligned” projects the visible tip shape deeper into the model.
  • Selections, hidden faces, symmetry and transparency lock work as usual. This option does not affect UV painting, which already reaches all faces.
On thick parts, the brush may reach the opposite side. Watch its size.

Detailed brush settings

  • The wavy stroke at the top previews current settings and updates immediately when they change.
  • Size basis: Per sub tool, choose Follow settings / Fixed on texture / Variable on screen. Fixed on texture keeps texture stroke width constant when zooming. Follow settings uses File → Preferences → Drawing → Brush size unit (screen px/texture px).
  • Tip: Round, square, flat brush, pencil, chalk, spray, watercolor, ink brush, sparkle, star, heart and 31 bundled tips. Import images (black shapes on white) or other painting apps' brush files (.abr, .gbr, .gih or bundled .zip).
  • Build-up: Repeated passes become darker (useful for airbrushes).
  • Rendering mode: 26 modes including Multiply, Screen and Overlay. Also includes Behind (unpainted areas only), Lighten and Darken.
  • Stabilization type: Smooth (follows with a delay), 1€ (stronger stabilization at low speed, less lag at high speed), or Tether (follows a little behind the tip to remove hand tremor).
  • Start/end taper: Narrows stroke starts and ends. End taper redraws the last segment when you lift the pen, avoiding lag while drawing.
  • Dynamics: Size by speed; size/opacity variation; size/opacity by tilt; tip rotation by pen direction or barrel rotation (tilt/rotation require a pen that reports them).
  • Pressure curve: Move points to set the response separately for size and opacity.
  • Paper texture: Paper or canvas grain modulates paint opacity (6 bundled images, 15 patterns or your own image). Stays attached to the surface as the view moves.
The Brush size panel on the left gives quick access to favorite sizes. Small tiles show circles and numbers; a narrow panel can become one column. Use the final pencil icon or right-click to add the current size or remove one.[ ] also changes size (1–1000).
3D brush projection: Surface-aligned reduces stretching on angled faces and is the v1.1.0 default for brushes without saved settings. Screen-aligned projects from the camera and stretches strokes on angled faces. Set per sub tool; saved settings remain intact. UV painting is unchanged.
Import external brush tips: use Import in Sub tool for ABR, GBR, GIH, images such as PNG, SUT, or ZIP. You can select multiple files or drop them onto the panel, then paint with the registered tools. Available in all editions.
Imports use tip images and available starting size, spacing, and similar values. They do not fully reproduce the original brush engine, pressure behavior, mixing, or colors. SUT imports use readable embedded PNG images, which may be previews or other materials. Adjust Tool properties after import.
ABR supports sampled tips in versions 1, 2, and 6; GBR supports versions 1 and 2. GIH tips are registered separately. Image tips use alpha or remove white. Importing and exporting .petabrush settings requires Standard or higher.
v1.2.0: Stroke feel (all editions): in File → Preferences → Pen & input → Stroke feel, choose “Taper by pressure only” (default) or “Taper by length” (the previous default). With pressure only, light pressure itself forms the taper, so strokes no longer start with a blob or end with a thread-like tail (pen only; mouse and touch always taper by length).
v1.2.0: If you used an earlier version, a one-time “stroke feel improved” notice appears at startup. “Restore previous feel” switches back to taper by length (you can also change it later in Preferences).

Blur, smudge and blend

  • Blur: Smooths the traced area. Blur width controls its range.
  • Smudge: Drags and stretches color.
  • Blend: Picks up and carries painted colors while blending them with the foreground color. At Paint amount 0, only existing colors are mixed. Increase Color stretch for longer extension.
All are calculated in 3D, remaining continuous across UV seams. Blur and smudge also work in UV (Blend is 3D only).
v1.2.0: Blur now also has “3D painting method” (follow surface / match screen). The default, follow surface, makes the blurred area match the pen shape.

Fill, gradients and shapes

  • Fill island: Fills the entire clicked UV island.Whole material: Fills the whole material.
  • Fill enclosed area: Click inside line art to fill connected similar-colored areas within that island. Set reference (all layers/selected layer), tolerance, Close gaps (stop at gaps in lines) and Expand (extend beneath lines).
  • Polygon fill: Fills traced faces entirely in the foreground color.
  • Gradient: Drag from start to end in 3D. With back/hidden-face painting enabled (default), follows the 3D line between pressed/released points onto back and inner faces too, for example from hair roots to tips.Shift constrains to 45° steps.
  • Shape: Draw lines, rectangles, ellipses and polygons (3–12 sides), filled or outlined.Shift constrains to squares/circles; Draw from center makes the starting point the center. For curves, drag and release, move to bend, then click again to finish. Point-to-point curves place a point per click; double-click or Enter to finish (Close makes a loop that can be filled).
Create the fill layer below the line-art layer to keep clean lines.

For multiple colors and opacity, see Color and opacity gradients.

Shape line width now follows brush size. Changing size from the brush size list, the [ ] keys, or the popup palette also changes shape line width (kept per shape sub tool, without changing the pen size).
v1.2.0: Lasso fill (all editions): a Fill sub tool. Drag around an area in 3D to fill it with the foreground color (the current selection is kept; one undo reverts it).
v1.2.0: Alt+Backspace fills with the foreground color and Ctrl+Backspace with the background color (inside the selection if any, otherwise the whole layer; masks use brightness). D (or the small button at the lower left of the two color squares) resets the colors to black and white.

Color and opacity gradients

The Gradient drawing tool is available in Lite and above. Under Color in Tool properties, select “Color and opacity stops” to draw with a gradient combining colors and transparency, beyond two colors. Foreground → background and foreground → transparent are also available.

  1. Choose a preset or click an empty part of the bar to add stops (2–32).
  2. Select a stop and change its color, position and opacity. Drag to reposition. Tap the selected stop again or double-click to choose its color.
  3. Remove unwanted stops with “Delete stop,” Delete or by dragging them away from the bar. Also use Insert foreground color, Distribute evenly and Reverse.
  4. Browse 240 presets by group/name, including transparent presets. The list scrolls. Name your gradient and use Save current to keep your own preset.
  5. Choose linear or radial, then drag from start to end in 3D or UV.
Gradient areas at opacity 0 preserve the colors underneath. To erase existing colors, use Draw with transparency. To bake height- or surface-direction shading onto new layers, use Pro's gradient bake is useful.

Paint a gradient from a point in 3D space

The regular gradient tool can spread color from a point placed in 3D space, including outside the model. Unlike the filter bake, it applies to the selected paint layer.

  1. Select the layer to paint, then click “Paint from a 3D point” in the gradient Tool properties. The “3D spatial gradient” dialog opens.
  2. Click a model surface or empty space in the 3D view to place the center point. Drag the movement handles or enter X, Y, and Z coordinates to move it.
  3. Use Radius to change how far the colors spread, and adjust colors with color/opacity stops or presets. Transparent areas retain the color underneath.
  4. Preview the result, then Apply to paint it on the selected layer. A single Undo returns to the state before applying. Cancel restores the layer’s original painting.

Distance and color range guides

Drag the outer white handle to change how far the colors spread. Colored handles move each color stop. The center movement handles and XYZ coordinates remain available, and handles, numeric controls, and color/opacity editing stay synchronized.

Rings show distance from the center; the rail shows color and opacity. Labels show each stop’s position, color, and opacity. A color marked ∅ does not appear within the current range. Press Esc during a drag to cancel that drag.

Use “Show range guide” to show or hide the rings, rail, and colored handles. The center movement handles remain available. Pro’s point-distance gradient bake uses the same guide, with ring and color positions reflecting changes to Boundary position and Blend width.

Existing linear and radial drag controls and UV gradients remain available. Use the Pro gradient bake for height- or surface-direction shading on new layers.

Choose colors and use the eyedropper

  • Color panel: switch among 5 pickers with the top buttons (ring + square, ring + triangle, square + hue bar, disc + lightness bar, sliders).
  • Color values panel: foreground/background color, HEX, screen eyedropper, RGB/HSV values and opacity.
  • Recent colors: 12 colors actually painted or picked. Click to set the foreground color.
  • X swaps foreground and background colors.
  • Eyedropper：Alt temporarily activates the eyedropper from any tool while held.
  • Pick color from screen(Shift＋I): pick anywhere on screen, including outside the window.
  • Color set: Tap a swatch to set the foreground color. Add with “+ Current foreground color”; drag to reorder. Swatch deletion can be undone. Import/export .gpl palettes too.
  • Reference image: Open, drop or paste (Ctrl＋V) an image, then pick colors with the eyedropper. Use the top magnifier to zoom, 100% for actual size and the corner marks to fit the whole image (pen-only operation supported).
Switch the auxiliary sliders with the RGB/HSV buttons. The upper color field and hue bar stay as they are. HSV adjusts hue, saturation, and brightness; your choice is retained after restart.
With Alt-button camera controls, Alt operates the camera in 3D. Use I for the 3D eyedropper; Alt still works as an eyedropper in UV.
When picking from a reference image, the cursor becomes an eyedropper with a white outline that stays visible on light and dark images. The tip marks the sampled position.

Draw with transparency (erase with the same brush)

In the Color values panel, use the checkerboard buttonor C to switch to transparent color. Pens, brushes and brush collections erase colors on the selected paint layer while retaining tip shape, hardness, pressure and flow.

Press C again or choose a regular foreground color to return. Transparency-locked layers cannot be erased; disable transparency lock first.

Transparent color switches brushes to erasing; it does not turn every tool into an eraser.
v1.2.0: Transparent color (all editions): fills (island, whole material, enclosed area), polygon fill, and shapes now erase when the color is transparent. Gradients erase more strongly toward the foreground side. Layers with Lock transparency are refused.

Paint symmetrically

  1. At the top left of the 3D view, click X (left-right), or press M .
  2. Draw as usual to paint the opposite side too.
  • Combine Y (front-back) and Z (top-bottom) too; X + Z paints in 4 places.
  • Use ▼ to choose Model center, Origin or Custom. For Custom, enter a value (cm) or click Set by clicking the model. Snap to vertex uses the vertex nearest the clicked point. Also available via 3D right-click → Paint symmetrically → Set symmetry center here.
  • For Model center, the left-right center is where the shape is most symmetrical (the body center even with one-sided decorations/accessories).
  • Under ▼, “Symmetry search range” (0–3%, default 1%) finds the closest opposite face within this range when the mirrored position has no face, such as slightly asymmetrical clothing.
  • The top of Tool properties and the UV view header also have Symmetry X/Y/Z (the same toggles as the 3D top left). Unsupported tools hide this section and cross out the 3D X/Y/Z buttons. Tools unsupported only in UV, such as Blend, show a reason.

Popup palette (pen-only controls)

  • Over 3D or UV, press the pen side button and release without moving to open a circular palette at the cursor (dragging still rotates).
  • Outer ring: favorite sub tools (+ adds the current sub tool; long-press/right-click removes; up to 10). Inside: hue ring/square for foreground color, small top circles for recent colors, and a bottom label dragged vertically for brush size.
  • Click outside the palette or press Esc to close. For mouse use, set File → Preferences → Controls → Right-click to Popup palette (also available as “Show popup palette” at the top of the right-click menu).
