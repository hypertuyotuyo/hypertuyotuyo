Interface, panels and preferences

This page covers where everything is on the screen, how to move panels and the toolbar, how to change the way the 3D view looks, and every item in Preferences. The panel layout comes back the next time you start the app.

Screenshots show the Japanese interface. Menu names in the text follow the selected app language.

Start with this
  1. To show a panel that is not showing, use Display → Windows (Details).
  2. Drag a panel's heading to move it wherever you like (Details).
  3. Change how the 3D view looks with the shading icon at the top left of the 3D view (Details).
  4. If you lose track of the layout, choose Display → Windows → "Reset window layout" (Details).
  5. Settings are in File → Preferences (Ctrl+Alt+Shift+K) (Details).

What is where on the screen (default layout)

This is the arrangement you get the first time you start the app. The middle is where you paint (the 3D view), with panels on the left and right.

  • Top: The menu bar. To the right of "File" are buttons for New, Open and Save, and for Undo and Redo (a button is dimmed when it cannot be used).
  • Left: The toolbar, plus Sub tool, Tool properties, Brush size and Reference image.
  • Right: Three columns. Right next to the 3D view are Texture set, Layer properties and Layer. Next to those are Color, Color values, Color set | Recent colors (as tabs) and History.
  • Far right: A column of Objects and the Pro panels (Vertex groups, Material, Blend shapes, Pose), folded into a strip of icons. Click an icon and that panel slides out to the side.

Layout that fits the window size

  • When the window is short (under 860 px high), the Texture set panel is collapsed. Switch sets from the heading of the Layer panel.
  • Under 1400 px wide (1280×720, 1366×768 and so on), the color column is also folded into the strip at the far right, which leaves more room for the 3D view. Under 1100 px wide, the columns get even narrower and Layer properties goes into the strip too.
  • If you are still on the default layout, it is rebuilt when you resize the window. After you change the layout, for example by moving or collapsing panels, it is not rebuilt.
  • The layout comes back the next time you start the app.

Return to the default layout

  1. Choose Display → Windows → "Reset window layout".
  2. The panels return to the default layout for the current window size. The toolbar goes back to the left, and the tools return to their original order.

If it doesn't work

  • All the panels and the toolbar are gone: They are hidden with Tab. Press Tab again, or choose Display → "Show/hide palettes".
  • I can't find the panel I want: Put a ✓ next to that panel in Display → Windows (Panel list). It may also be folded into the strip of icons at the far right.
  • The layout doesn't change when I enlarge the window: After you change the layout, it is not rebuilt. Use "Reset window layout" to fit it to the current size.

Show and hide panels (panel list)

Show and hide panels with Display → Windows in the menu. A panel with a ✓ is showing.

  1. Open Display → Windows.
  2. Choose the name of the panel you want. A ✓ appears and the panel shows.
  3. To hide it, choose it again. You can also hide it by right-clicking the panel's heading → "Hide this panel".

Move panels

You can arrange panels in as many columns and rows as you like: on the left and right of the 3D view (vertical columns) and above and below it (horizontal rows). You can also leave a panel floating, without docking it anywhere.

  1. Drag a panel's heading. A line or frame shows where it will go.
  2. Release where you want it. It is placed where you release.
Where you releaseWhat happens
The middle of a column or rowInserts the panel into that column or row (a line shows where it goes; within a row, panels sit side by side)
The edge of a column or rowMakes a new column or row next to it (a thick line appears at the edge)
The frames at the edges of the 3D view ("Add column on the left", "Add column on the right", "Place above", "Place below")Makes a new column or row on that side
Anywhere else (such as the middle of the 3D view)It becomes a floating window

Change width and height

  • Drag the border between columns, or between rows, to change the width or height. Double-click a border to restore it.
  • For panels in the left and right columns, drag the handle at the bottom edge to change the height (double-click to return to automatic). Long contents scroll inside the panel.
  • Empty columns and rows disappear, and the 3D view gets wider.
  • The tiles in Sub tool and Brush size rearrange to fit the width and height of the panel. A column or floating window with only Brush size can be narrowed to the width of one column of tiles (about 64 px).

Floating windows

  • Drag the heading to move it, and use the handle at the bottom right to resize it. Click the heading to collapse it.
  • There is no ×. To hide it, use Display → Windows, or right-click the heading → "Hide this panel".
  • Panels stay inside the app window. They cannot go into a separate window outside the app.

Lock the layout

  • Put a ✓ on Display → Windows → "Lock panel layout" to stop panels from being moved or resized. This prevents accidental moves.
  • Even while the layout is locked, you can still move and resize floating windows by dragging the heading.
  • With the lock at the top right of a floating window, you can lock or unlock just that window. When it is locked, it does not move or change size (this is remembered the next time you open it).

Collapse panels and group them in tabs

Making unused panels small gives the other panels and the 3D view more room.

Toolbar and favorite tools

The toolbar starts on the left. You can attach it above, below or on the right of the work area, or let it float.

  1. Drag the toolbar's handle (the top edge when it is vertical, the left edge when it is horizontal). Docking targets appear on the four sides of the work area.
  2. Release on a target to attach the toolbar to that edge. At the top or bottom, the tools line up horizontally and wrap if they do not fit.
  • If you release anywhere else, it floats. A floating toolbar switches between "Horizontal" and "Vertical", and double-clicking the handle returns it to its previous edge.
  • You can also choose Left, Right, Top, Bottom or Float from a button's right-click menu → "Toolbar position". The position is remembered the next time you start the app.
  • Right-click a button → "Toolbar icon size" to choose large or small, and "Toolbar columns" to choose "Columns: Auto" or "Columns: Always 1". Tools that do not fit can be scrolled with the wheel, the arrows at the edge, or by dragging with the pen.
  • Reorder: Drag the buttons (a pen works too) into any order. Release after moving a little to reorder, or release without moving to select that tool. Right-click → "Reset order" returns them to the original order.
  • Theme color: The sun and moon switch at the very end of the toolbar toggles between Light and Dark (Dark mode).
  • Hide: Remove the ✓ from Display → Windows → "Toolbar".
  • Toolbox: Display → Windows → "Toolbox" shows a panel with the tool icons in a grid. Like other panels, you can put it in a column or tab, and it wraps to fit the width. Click the foreground color below it to open the Color panel, click the background color or ⇄ to swap them, and click the small black-and-white icon or press D to return to black and white.

Favorite tools strip

Line up your favorite tools and sub tools in a small strip above the 3D view and the UV view, so you can switch to them quickly.

  1. Right-click a toolbar button or a sub tool (brush collection presets too).
  2. Choose "Add to favorites". An icon is added to the strip.
  3. Click an icon in the strip to switch to that tool or sub tool.
  • Use "+" in the strip to add the current sub tool, and "-" to remove it if it is already there (this works with a pen alone too). You can also remove an icon by right-clicking it.
  • Move the strip with the dotted handle on the left, and drag icons to reorder them.
  • Right-click the strip for "Arrange vertically", "Reset position" and "Hide this bar". × also hides it.
  • Show a hidden strip again with Display → Windows → "Favorite tools (3D view)" or "Favorite tools (UV view)", each separately.

Change the field of view

With the ▼ on the perspective/orthographic icon at the top right, you can change the projection, and set the field of view of the perspective view from 5 to 120°. The smaller it is, the more telephoto the view; the larger it is, the wider the angle. "Reset to defaults" returns it to 30°.

Move the view with the 3D navigator

The 3D navigator lets you move the view without using mouse buttons or keys. It is handy when you draw with only a pen.

  • You can also change the view with the numpad (Views (numpad)).

Lock the camera direction

Click the padlock button to stop only the rotation of the view. Pan and zoom still work. Click it again to unlock. Use this when you want to move in close and move around while keeping the direction you chose.

Tilt the screen (view rotation)

You can tilt the 3D view like a sheet of paper, to an angle that is easy to draw at.

  • Shift+wheel rotates it 5° at a time, and ⟲ and ⟳ on the 3D navigator rotate it 15° at a time. You can also drag the round arrow left and right.
  • If the cursor is over the UV pane, it rotates the UV.
  • While the view is rotated, painting, selecting, panning and right-drag rotation all work as usual. Drags move in the direction you see.
  • In Key bindings, you can also assign keys to "Rotate view left (15°)", "Rotate view right (15°)" and "Reset view rotation" (no keys at first).

Move or shrink the navigator

  • Drag the handle (…) at the top to move it anywhere. Double-click it to return the navigator to the top right.
  • Drag the left edge, the bottom edge or the bottom-left corner to resize it. You can also change it with "3D navigator size" in File → Preferences → Interface.
  • Click "—" to shrink it into an icon at the bottom right. Click the icon to bring it back (or use Display → Windows → "3D navigator (bring it back with the icon at bottom right)").
  • You can show rotation and zoom sliders below the navigator. Turn on "Show rotation/zoom sliders on the navigator" in File → Preferences → Interface (off at first). They appear on both the 3D navigator and the UV guide.

View another angle at the same time in a small window

Shows a small 3D view from a second camera inside the 3D view. While you draw, you can check how it looks from the opposite side or from the side. The small window is for viewing only. You draw in the large view.

  1. Click the alternate-angle mini view icon at the bottom right of the 3D view (or use Display → Windows → "Alternate-angle mini view").
  2. Choose the direction (Front, Right, Back, Left, Top, Opposite) from the small window's heading. "Opposite" is the opposite side of the viewpoint of the large view.
  • Drag inside the small window to rotate it, and pan with the middle button or Shift+drag.
  • Zoom in and out with the wheel or Ctrl+drag (drag up to zoom in; handy with a pen), and double-click for Frame all.
  • Move it by its heading, and resize it from the left edge, the top edge and the top-left corner. Close it with ✕.

Put 3D and UV in a small window

When 3D and UV are shown side by side, you can turn one of them into a small window and give the other more room.

  1. Press U to show UV next to 3D (Show the UV pane).
  2. Click the button under the ✕ at the top right of the one you want as a small window. That one becomes a small window, and the other fills the whole area.
  • You can do the same with Display → UV → "UV in a small window (3D full)" and "3D in a small window (UV full)", or from the ⋯ in the UV heading.
  • Move the small window by dragging its heading, and resize it from its four sides and four corners. Inside the app window, you can also place it over the panels.
  • ⇄ swaps the small window and the full view, ▣ returns to the side-by-side display, and ✕ closes the small window.
  • Lock it with the padlock at the top right (it will not move or change size). Even if you hide the palettes with Tab, the small window stays.

Change how it looks (shading and light direction)

Switches how the 3D view looks. Only the look changes. The picture you painted and the images you export do not change.

  1. Click the shading icon at the top left of the 3D view (you can also choose it from Display → Shading).
  2. Choose the shading you want. The app opens with the same shading next time.

Single-sided and double-sided display

Choose from the same row of icons, or from Display → Shading. The choices are "Single-sided display (back faces are not drawn)" and "Double-sided display (paint visible back faces too)". When you paint the back side, such as the inside of clothing, use double-sided (Also paint back and hidden faces).

Change the light direction

  1. Click the ▼ below the shading icon.
  2. Drag the sphere to set the direction the light comes from. You can also change it with the handles for horizontal angle and height.
  3. Turn on "Follow camera" and the light falls the same way even when you rotate the view. "Reset to default" restores the original.
  • Two-tone toon, Shaded and Final look use the same light. In "Painted colors as-is", you cannot see the light.
  • Pro These are the same values as "Display and lighting" in the Material panel.
  • When the camera control is "Alt + mouse buttons", Shift+right-drag rotates the light horizontally.

How transparent areas look

  • Areas you painted with the Eraser or a transparent color show a checkerboard underneath in 3D. Turn off Display → Shading → "Show transparent areas as a checkerboard" and they turn gray.
  • Right-click a texture set row (or "…") → "Alpha cutout (hide transparency in 3D)" to choose how transparency is shown: "None", "Cutout" (areas fainter than 50% opacity are cut out so you can see through them) and "Transparent" (see-through at the actual opacity).
  • Alpha cutout also changes only how it looks, and the exported images do not change. It is kept in the project file and can be undone. You cannot click cut-out areas in 3D, so paint them in the UV pane.

Show textures as pixels

Shows the texture in 3D without interpolation, as one square per pixel. Areas that look fine when zoomed out are still interpolated.

  • Switch it for each texture set, using ⋯ on the set's row or right-click → "Show as pixels (no interpolation)". A set that has it on shows a square badge on its row (click the badge to turn it off).
  • Every set has it on from the start. Sets you switched yourself are kept in the project file. This only affects the display; your painting and exports do not change.
  • Display → Shading → "Show all sets' textures as pixels (no interpolation)" switches all the sets at once.
  • The UV pane always shows pixels when you zoom in.

If it doesn't work

  • Shadows make the painted colors hard to see: Set the shading to "Painted colors as-is".
  • Changing the light direction changes nothing: You cannot see the light in "Painted colors as-is". Use Two-tone toon or Shaded.
  • Areas I made transparent look gray or like a checkerboard: That is only how they look. To see through them, set the texture set's "Alpha cutout" to Cutout or Transparent.
  • I turned on Alpha cutout and now I cannot paint there: You cannot click cut-out areas in 3D. Paint them in the UV pane, or set Alpha cutout back to "None".
  • A low-resolution texture looks blurry in 3D: Turn on "Show as pixels (no interpolation)" for that set.
  • I cannot choose "Final look": It is shown only in Pro (Edition comparison).

  • If I hold a button down, a menu appears and I cannot press it: In the same place, set "Long press on buttons" to "Release to confirm (default)".

Popup palette (pen-only controls)

A round palette that lets you change the color, sub tool and brush size on the spot, while you keep holding the pen.

  1. Over the 3D view or the UV pane, press the pen's side button.
  2. Release it without moving the pen. The round palette appears right there (if you move the pen, it rotates or pans as before).
  3. When you are done, click outside the palette (it does not draw). Esc or tapping the right button again also closes it.
  • Outer ring: Your favorite sub tools. Click one to switch to it and close the palette. Use "+" to add the current sub tool, and long-press or right-click to remove one (up to 10).
  • Inside: Choose the foreground color with the hue ring and the saturation/brightness square. The small circles at the top are recent colors.
  • Tab at the bottom: Drag up and down to change the brush size.
  • To open it with the mouse, set "Right-click" to "Popup palette" in File → Preferences → Controls → Mouse (it is "Menu" at first). You can also open it with "Show popup palette" at the top of the right-click menu.
  • If you turned off Windows Ink in your pen tablet's settings, the side button also arrives as a mouse right-click, so it follows the "Right-click" setting above.

Draw while looking at a reference image

Show a reference image in a panel and draw while looking at it. You can also pick colors from the image.

  1. Click "Open" in the Reference image panel and choose an image.
  2. Switch between the images you opened with the thumbnails below. Close one with ×.
  • You can also open one with File → Import → "Open reference image", by dropping a file onto the panel, or by clicking the panel and pressing Ctrl+V.
  • Pick colors: With the Eyedropper, click or drag on the image. When you release, that color goes into Recent colors.
  • Move: Pan with the hand, the middle button, the right button or Space+drag, and zoom with the wheel.
  • Display: The buttons at the top give you Frame all, flip horizontally and rotate 90°. Zoom with the + and - of the magnifier (these work with a pen alone), and "100%" shows one pixel of the image as one point on the screen.
  • Images are saved with the long side reduced to 1600 px, and they come back the next time you start the app.

Common controls

These controls work the same way in every panel and window.

  • Descriptions of buttons and items appear when you rest the cursor (or the pen tip) over the name.
  • You can move the Preferences, Shortcut list and confirmation windows by dragging their headings. Close them with the × at the top right or with Esc.
  • Menu items and buttons are also confirmed when you press down with the pen and then release (you can change this in Pen & input).
  • A pen double-tap counts as a double-click even if the second tap lands slightly off (within 12 px and 0.5 seconds).
  • The eraser end of the pen also works the same as the pen tip for panel and window controls (buttons, sliders, reordering, dragging). Inside the drawing area, it acts as an eraser.
  • Browser shortcut keys (reload, print, page zoom and so on) do not work. Text on the screen cannot be selected (text fields can).
  • Features that are not available in your edition have dimmed menus and buttons with a lock icon (Edition comparison).

Change dark mode, colors and language

Switches the screen colors between a bright "Light" and a dark "Dark".

  1. Click the sun and moon switch at the very end of the toolbar (the bottom when it is vertical, the right end when it is horizontal).
  2. Light and Dark switch.
  • File → Preferences → Interface → Display → "Theme color" does the same (Light at first).
  • To switch with a key, assign a key to "Dark mode" in the "Display" category of Key bindings (Assign keys).

Change the color scheme

In File → Preferences → Interface → Display → "Color scheme", you can change the colors of the background, panels, buttons, text, accent color, secondary color, 3D background, UV background and toolbar.

  • Light and Dark are saved separately.
  • "Reset colors to defaults" returns to the original colors.

Change the display language

Choose from "Language" under File → Preferences → Interface → Language. The choices are Japanese (日本語), English, Korean (한국어), Chinese Simplified (中文（简体）) and Chinese Traditional (中文（繁體）).

Preferences

These are the settings for the whole app. Open them with File → Preferences (Ctrl+Alt+Shift+K).

  • Categories are on the left and sections are on the right. Click a section heading to open or collapse it (a collapsed section stays collapsed next time).
  • Rest the cursor on a name to see a description of the item.
  • Settings are kept the next time you start the app.

  • How tapering is made: "Taper by pressure only" makes light pressure thin sharply, and also tapers the start and end of a line by pressure (a mouse always uses length). "Taper by length" tapers the ends by the "Taper start" and "Taper end" lengths of the sub tool.
  • Show pen strokes early: Shows the line you are drawing early, in a transparent overlay window, which speeds up what you see by about one frame. It works only when you draw with a pen (Windows Ink) using a round, hard pen or brush. While it is on, the "Predicted preview line" is not shown.
  • Predicted preview line: Draws the end of the line you are drawing tentatively, as far as the predicted next movement, to hide lag. It is only for hard round pens.
  • Long press on buttons: With "Release to confirm", a long press is confirmed when you release (open the menu with the pen side button). With "Long press opens the right-click menu", a long press opens the menu. In places that are not buttons, such as layer rows, a long press opens the menu either way.
  • Pen side buttons are covered in Pen buttons and Windows Ink.

Settings that are not in Preferences

The following settings are found only where you use them.

  • Paint across two sets: "Paint across" at the bottom left of the 3D view (Main and sub)
  • Keep painting outside UV islands: the heading of the UV pane, and Display → UV (When strokes go beyond an island)
  • Move layers and sub tools by dragging with the pen: "…" on Layer
  • Export padding and destination: the export window (Export bleed)
  • Mirror partner search range: the ▼ next to Symmetry at the top left of the 3D view (Paint symmetrically)

If it doesn't work

  • There is no "3D mouse" section: It does not appear until you have connected a 3D mouse once. It appears when you connect one.
  • I turned on "Show pen strokes early" but nothing changes: It works only when you draw with a pen (Windows Ink). It does nothing with a mouse, with touch, or with a pen that uses "Read pen pressure directly (WinTab)". If it could not be started, the reason is shown at the bottom right and the setting turns off.
  • Key zoom goes to the center of the screen: Turn on "Key zoom centers on cursor" in Controls → Camera.
